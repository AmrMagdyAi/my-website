Adding acquisitions reps? Set this up in Salesforce before their first call

Adding a rep usually goes like this: a login, a dialer seat, a couple of days listening to calls, and then leads. The first weeks are when leads slip the most, and nobody notices until the owner asks about a seller who never got a callback. This checklist is for owners and acquisitions managers on Salesforce who are about to hire. It's what I'd want in place before a new rep's first call, plus two short check-ins after.

Short answer

Before day one, give the rep their own user with a sales profile and a permission set, under their manager in the role hierarchy. Connect their phone, texting and email to that user, add them to lead assignment only from their first calling day, and give them three list views and approved templates. In the first week, add a speed-to-lead alert and required fields by stage, and read their leads on day three. After two weeks, compare them with the team on a ramp report, and have a plan ready for the day a rep leaves.

Three things people forget in this part:

  • A license. Every rep needs their own Salesforce user license. If someone has left, deactivating their user frees that license. Freezing them doesn't.
  • Multi-factor authentication. Salesforce enforces MFA for all employee logins, so set it up with the rep before their first calling day, not at 8 a.m. on it.
  • The round robin itself. If yours is a formula, such as MOD(VALUE(Lead_Number__c), 3) for three reps, a fourth rep means changing the 3 to a 4 and adding a rule for the new value. Do it on the rep's first calling day, and create a test lead afterwards. More options in lead and call routing.

If the new person is a VA or a cold caller rather than an acquisitions rep, they need a narrower setup. See VA access in Salesforce.

Compare a new rep with your own team over the same number of weeks, not with a benchmark from the internet. Lead flow, sources and markets differ too much between teams for an outside number to mean much. What you're looking for is the trend. Activity numbers, like contacts and next steps, are the ones a new rep controls, so they should catch up with the team first. Offers and contracts take longer.

If the rep's leads keep showing up on the "no next step" report, that's a coaching conversation, not a Salesforce problem. If everyone's leads show up there, it's the follow-up process. See the follow-up cadence builder.

When a rep leaves

Step 11 on the checklist is a plan for the day a rep leaves, written before you need it. Reps leave, and the leads they owned still belong to you. In order:

  1. Reassign their open leads, deals and tasks the same day. Every seller history stays on the record, so the next rep can read it before calling.
  2. Take them out of everything that sends them work: round robin, assignment rules, queues, alert recipients, scheduled reports and any dashboard that runs as them.
  3. Freeze the user, so they can't log in while you finish the cleanup. Freezing doesn't free the license.
  4. Deactivate the user once nothing depends on them. If Salesforce won't let you, something still points at them, such as a default lead owner setting or an automation. That's useful to know, and worth fixing.
  5. Close the side doors: their dialer, texting and email seats, and any phone number sellers know them by. If that number stays live, point it at someone who can answer.

Practice this once on a test user, so the first time isn't the day someone leaves. If the person leaving is the one who runs Salesforce, it's a bigger job. See what to do when your Salesforce admin leaves.

Where a new rep's leads usually slip

  • A shared login "until the license comes through". Nothing they do is tracked to them, and every report on that user is wrong for weeks.
  • Texting sellers from a personal cell. The conversation never reaches Salesforce, and it leaves with the rep. Every text should go through your texting tool. More in texting in Salesforce.
  • Admin rights "to save time". One wrong click edits an automation for the whole team.
  • Leads before the phone works. The first leads wait while someone fixes the dialer connection.
  • No manager above them in the role hierarchy, so the person accountable for the rep can't see the rep's leads.
  • Missed calls with nowhere to go. If the rep owns a seller who calls back while they're on another call, a missed-call alert should reach them, or a queue. See missed-call alerts in Salesforce.

Questions

What should I set up in Salesforce before a new acquisitions rep starts?

Their own user with a sales profile and permission set under their manager, MFA, phone, texting and email connected to their user, lead assignment starting on their first calling day, three list views (new leads, no activity in 48 hours, follow-ups due today) and approved templates.

Should a new acquisitions rep get leads on their first day?

Only once their phone, texting and email are connected and tested, and they know the follow-up process. Add them to lead assignment from the day they start taking calls, not the day their user is created. Some teams start a new rep on a smaller share of leads and widen it later.

Can two reps share one Salesforce login?

They shouldn't. Each Salesforce license is for one named user, nothing either rep does can be told apart in reports, and with MFA enforced, sharing a login means sharing one person's phone or authenticator app. Give each rep their own user from day one.

Do I need a new Salesforce license for every rep?

Yes, each user needs their own license. If a former rep's user is still active, deactivating it frees that license for the new rep. Freezing a user blocks their login but doesn't free the license.

What permissions should an acquisitions rep have in Salesforce?

A sales profile plus a permission set with what the job needs: their leads and deals, the dialer and texting tools, and their reports. No admin rights, no export and no delete. Their manager should sit above them in the role hierarchy so the manager sees their records.

How do I know if a new rep is ramping well?

Run a ramp report with leads assigned, contacted, appointments and offers for each rep, next to the team average over the same weeks. Watch the trend and the list of their open leads with no next step, rather than comparing them with a number from outside your team.

What happens to a rep's leads when they leave?

Reassign their open leads, deals and tasks the same day, take them out of round robin, queues and alerts, freeze their user, then deactivate it once nothing depends on it. The seller history stays on each record for the next rep to read.
