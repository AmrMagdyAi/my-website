Salesforce for UK property investors and deal sourcers: the same deal system, set up for UK rules

I build Salesforce for property businesses that buy direct from owners. If you run an investing or deal sourcing business in the UK, two questions matter before anything else: does the system fit how you work, and what has to change? This page answers both, for buy-to-let, HMO and BRR investors, deal sourcers and packagers. I work remotely, and UK afternoons sit inside my working day.

Short answer

The core is the same as for a US home-buying company: every vendor lead answered fast, offers and their numbers on the deal record, investors matched to the right deals, and ROI counted on deals that actually complete. What changes in the UK is the stages and the consent rules. Build the pipeline around offer accepted, solicitors instructed, searches, mortgage offer, exchange and completion. Screen cold-call lists against the TPS, get consent before marketing texts and emails, and record all of it in the CRM. Set GBP as the currency, and track sourcing fees from invoice to payment.

What stays the same?

Whether you buy for your own portfolio or source for investors, the system underneath is the one a US home-buying company runs:

  • Every vendor lead captured with its source, from letters, leaflets, portals, PPC and referrals, and every missed call turned into a callback task. See missed-call alerts in Salesforce.
  • Offers and the numbers behind them on the deal record, so you can see why a deal was won or lost, instead of digging through WhatsApp.
  • One rule for which campaign gets credit when a vendor has had three letters and a leaflet. See lead attribution for investors.
  • ROI counted on deals that complete, not offers accepted.

The full picture is in my Salesforce for real estate investors guide. It's written in US terms, so read "seller" as vendor and "closing" as completion.

Deal sourcing: investors, packs and fees

If you source deals for investors, the CRM has a second side, much like dispositions in a US wholesaling business:

  • Investors stored with what they buy: strategy (buy-to-let, HMO, BRR, serviced accommodation), areas, budget, and cash or mortgage.
  • Every deal pack logged against the investor and the deal, so you can see who's had it and who replied.
  • Matching. A new deal goes first to investors whose areas, strategy and budget fit, ranked by what they've already bought from you. See dispositions in Salesforce.
  • Sourcing fees tracked from agreement to invoice to payment, with the date each step happened.

Check whether your business needs to register with HMRC for anti-money laundering supervision and join a property redress scheme. In December 2025, The Negotiator reported industry research finding that almost all property-sourcing agents were breaching anti-money laundering rules. Whatever applies to you, the CRM is where the ID checks and the records should live, so you can show them when asked.

Cold calling: the TPS

Treat calls offering to buy someone's property as marketing calls unless an adviser tells you otherwise. Under the ICO's guidance on the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR):

  • Don't make live marketing calls to numbers registered with the Telephone Preference Service (TPS) unless the person has agreed to your calls, and screen your call lists against it.
  • Keep your own do-not-call list of people who object or opt out, and screen against that too.
  • Always say who's calling, and let your number display.
  • Automated, recorded marketing calls need consent that specifically covers them.

What that means in the CRM:

  • Every list is screened before it's loaded, and each number carries the date it was checked and the result.
  • An objection flag follows the person across every record, including duplicates. See duplicate sellers in Salesforce.
  • A report of calls to flagged numbers, which should always be empty.

Texts and emails: PECR

The ICO says you must not send marketing texts or emails to individuals without specific consent. The soft opt-in covers existing customers who bought, or negotiated to buy, something similar from you, so it won't usually cover a vendor you've never dealt with. Every message has to say who it's from and give a simple way to opt out, and people who opt out go on a suppression list.

In the CRM, that means consent recorded with the date and where it came from, opt-outs that stop every automation at once, and texts sent through a tool that logs to Salesforce, never from someone's own phone. None of this is legal advice.

UK GDPR

UK GDPR covers the personal data you hold on vendors and investors. The CRM side is practical: record where each person's details came from, limit exports to owners and ops, decide how long you keep dead leads, and be able to find, export or delete one person's data when they ask. Get the details from a data protection adviser. For limiting what callers and VAs can see, see VA access in Salesforce.

GBP, areas and portals

  • Currency. Set the org's corporate currency to GBP. If you also buy abroad, Salesforce's multiple currencies feature lets each deal carry its own currency. It can't be turned off once enabled, so decide before you build.
  • Areas. Store the postcode district on every lead, deal and investor. It's the simplest key for matching deals to investors and splitting reports by area.
  • Sources. Give each letter campaign, leaflet drop, portal and PPC campaign its own tracking number or landing page, so every lead arrives with its source.
  • Tax and legal figures. I don't give tax or legal advice. If you want stamp duty or other figures in your reports, store the numbers your accountant gives you on the deal.

Does Salesforce work for UK property investors?

Yes. The differences are in setup: a pipeline with offer accepted, solicitors, searches, mortgage offer, exchange and completion; GBP as the corporate currency; postcode districts for matching; and TPS checks, consent and opt-outs recorded on every person.

Do I need to screen cold-call lists against the TPS?

If your calls count as marketing, yes. The ICO's guidance says not to make live marketing calls to TPS-registered numbers without consent, to screen call lists against the TPS and your own do-not-call list, and to say who is calling. Treat calls offering to buy a property as marketing unless an adviser tells you otherwise.

Can I text vendors without their consent?

Under PECR, marketing texts and emails to individuals need specific consent. The soft opt-in only covers existing customers who bought or negotiated to buy something similar from you, so it usually won't cover a new vendor. Every message must say who it's from and include a way to opt out.

Can Salesforce track sourcing fees and match deals to investors?

Yes. Store each investor's strategy, areas, budget and finance type, log every deal pack sent, match new deals to investors who fit, and track each sourcing fee from agreement to invoice to payment.
