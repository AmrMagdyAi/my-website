Seller follow-up messages: 12 templates, and the rules that decide when they send

Most wholesalers don't have a follow-up problem, they have a message problem and a trigger problem. The messages sound like a form letter, and they go out when someone remembers rather than when the seller's situation calls for them. This page gives you twelve short templates for the moments that matter (a missed call, a confirmed appointment, a quiet offer, a seller who said "not now"), written to sound like a person typed them. Under each is the rule that should send it, so the CRM does the remembering and a human does the talking. Timing for the whole sequence is in the cadence builder; this page is the words and the triggers.

Short answer

Keep each message short, from a named person, with one question or one next step. Send a text only to sellers who've agreed to be texted, identify yourself, and honor STOP. Let the CRM trigger each message from an event (missed call, appointment set, offer sent, re-contact date reached), stop the sequence the moment the seller replies, and log every message on the record. Use the templates below as a starting point and change the words to sound like you.

What rules should every message follow?

  • Short and human. Text messages of one to three sentences. Written the way you'd text a neighbor, not a lead.
  • One ask. A question they can answer in a word, or a single next step. Never two.
  • No pressure and no promises. Don't state prices, timelines or terms you haven't agreed. Don't invent urgency.
  • Name yourself and your company in the first message to someone, and include an easy way to stop (a "reply STOP" line) in texts, as your texting provider requires.
  • Consent first for texts. A skip-traced number is not agreement to be texted. See texting sellers: 10DLC and consent. If you're unsure, call or send mail instead and ask for text permission on the call.
  • Honor opt-outs everywhere. One STOP covers every duplicate record and every channel you control. Opt-outs must be processed within 10 business days at the latest under the FCC's rules since April 2025; do it immediately.
  • Quiet hours. Send in the seller's daytime, in their time zone.

This is information, not legal advice; confirm the rules for your states and your texting provider. Merge fields appear as {first_name}, {address}, {rep_name} and {company}.

Missed call and new lead

1. Missed call text-back (sends within minutes, only with consent)

Hi {first_name}, it's {rep_name} with {company}. Sorry I missed your call. Are you still looking to sell {address}? Reply STOP to opt out.

Trigger: missed inbound call from a known seller who has agreed to texts. Owner: the lead owner, who also gets a callback task. Stops when: the seller replies or a person calls them.

2. First reply after a web form or ad lead

Hi {first_name}, {rep_name} here from {company}. Got your note about {address}. Good time for a quick call today, or is later better?

Trigger: new lead with consent recorded on the form. Stops when: they reply or a call connects.

3. After the first conversation

Thanks for talking with me, {first_name}. I'll {next_step} by {date}. If anything changes before then, text me here.

Trigger: outcome set to "spoke, interested" with a next step and a date. The text uses the fields the caller filled in, which is another reason to require them.

Appointment

4. Appointment confirmation

Confirming {day} at {time} at {address}. Anyone else who helps decide, like a spouse or heir, is welcome. See you then, {rep_name}.

Trigger: appointment created. A second reminder the morning of, from the same rule. Asking about other decision-makers here is a system trick: it fills the "who else decides" field before the visit.

5. No-show or missed appointment

Hi {first_name}, we missed each other today. No problem. Want to pick a new time, or should I check back in a few weeks?

Trigger: appointment time passed with no outcome. Gives the seller an easy way out and keeps the lead alive. More in seller no-shows.

Offer

6. Offer sent, day one

Hi {first_name}, I just emailed the offer for {address}. Any questions on it, text or call me. I'm around today.

Trigger: offer date set. Also creates the follow-up task with a date; the pipeline needs that date to leave the stage (pipeline stages).

7. Offer gone quiet

Hi {first_name}, checking back on the offer for {address}. Is there anything I can answer, or is the timing off right now?

Trigger: a set number of days after the offer with no activity. Gives two honest ways to answer, and the second ("timing") feeds your not-now reason field. More in offer follow-up.

8. Seller says yes

Great, {first_name}. I'll send the agreement now. Once it's signed, I'll introduce you to {tc_name}, who will handle the title side.

Trigger: stage moved to Under contract. Starts dispo and the transaction checklist on the same day.

Not now and reactivation

9. Not-now check-in on the re-contact date

Hi {first_name}, it's {rep_name}. You mentioned {date_reason} would be the time to talk about {address}. Is now better?

Trigger: re-contact date reached. The reason field written weeks ago is what makes this feel like memory rather than automation.

10. Reactivation wave for a quiet lead

Hi {first_name}, {rep_name} with {company}. We spoke a while back about {address}. Is it still something you're thinking about?

Trigger: a person-approved segment, in small waves, consent confirmed. Never run on the whole database at once; see reactivating dead seller leads.

Voicemail

11. Voicemail script (about 15 seconds)

Hi {first_name}, this is {rep_name} with {company}, calling about {address}. No pressure. If it's still something you're considering, call or text me back at {number}. Thanks.

Trigger: call outcome "left voicemail". Say the number slowly, once at the end. Don't leave a voicemail on every attempt; one on the first and another much later is plenty.

Email

12. Email after the offer

Subject: Your offer for {address}

Hi {first_name}, thanks for your time. The offer is attached. In short: {offer_summary}. If something in it doesn't work, tell me which part and I'll see what I can do. I'll check back on {date}. {rep_name}, {number}

Trigger: offer sent. Email is the place for detail, and it leaves a record the seller can show a family member.

How should the CRM send them?

  1. Trigger from events, not from calendars. Each template above starts from something that happened: a missed call, an appointment, an offer date, a re-contact date.
  2. Check consent and opt-out before every send, in the same step that sends.
  3. Stop on reply. Any inbound text, call or email ends the automated sequence and creates a task for the owner.
  4. One channel per touch. Don't text, email and call within the same hour. It reads as pressure and as spam.
  5. Log it all on the seller's record: what, when and how they responded, so the next person doesn't repeat it.
  6. Keep templates in one place with an owner. Edit them as a team, review replies monthly and retire the ones that get "stop" responses.

The same pieces also tell you what to ask of an AI tool that drafts messages: the triggers, the consent check and the stop rule stay yours. See AI for wholesalers. When to send what, across weeks, is in the follow-up cadence builder.

Questions

What should I text a motivated seller after a missed call?

Keep it short and human: say who you are, that you missed their call, and ask one question, such as whether they're still looking to sell the property. Only text people who have agreed to be texted, and include a way to opt out.

How do I follow up with a seller who went quiet after an offer?

Send a short message a few days after the offer asking whether you can answer anything or whether the timing is off, and record their answer in a reason field. Make the follow-up date required when the offer is sent so it never depends on memory.

Do I need consent to text seller leads?

Generally yes. A skip-traced phone number isn't permission to text. Get consent on the form or during a call, record it on the record, include opt-out language and check the rules for your states. This is information, not legal advice.

What should a follow-up voicemail for a seller say?

Say your name and company, the property you're calling about, that there's no pressure, and your number, slowly, once. Keep it around 15 seconds, and don't leave one on every attempt.

How often should I send follow-up messages to sellers?

Space them by event and by seller type rather than a fixed rhythm, use one channel per touch, and stop the automation when the seller replies. The cadence builder gives a touch plan by seller type.

Can AI write my seller follow-up messages?

It can draft them for a person to review, which is a low-risk use. The triggers, the consent check, opt-out handling and the rule that stops a sequence on reply should stay in your CRM, whatever writes the words.
