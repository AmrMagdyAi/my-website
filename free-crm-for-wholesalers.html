Free CRM for wholesalers: what free gets you and where it stops working

If you're searching for a free CRM, you're probably right to start small. One person, a few leads a week and a spreadsheet can run a wholesaling business for a while. The trouble is that free tools are built for a general business, and wholesaling puts strain on exactly the places where free plans are thin: lots of records at once, several people touching the same seller, calls and texts that need to be logged and honored, and a need to know which marketing produced a contract. This guide is a plain look at what a free setup can do, the limits that bite first, and the signs it is time to move. I build Salesforce for home-buying teams, so I'll say up front that for a solo wholesaler, free is often the right answer.

Short answer

A free CRM or a spreadsheet works while one person handles a few dozen new leads a month. It stops working when a second or third person (a VA, a cold caller, an acquisitions rep) needs the same seller records, when you load lists of thousands of rows, when you need automatic follow-up tasks and call or text logging, or when you need to know which campaign produced which contract. Check six things before you commit: user limit, record limit, automation, call and text logging, reporting by source, and whether you can export everything.

What does "free CRM" actually mean?

Three different things get called free, and the difference matters:

  • A spreadsheet. Free forever, fully yours, and it does exactly what you build into it. My seller lead tracker shows how far one can go.
  • A free tier of a paid CRM. A real CRM with caps on users, records, automation or reporting. The vendor expects you to grow into the paid plan.
  • A free trial. Free for a few weeks, then it bills. Useful for testing, not a place to run a business.

Free tiers change, so check the vendor's page before you build on one. As of the pages I checked in October 2026, HubSpot's free CRM allows up to 2 users, 1,000 contacts and one automated email action, and Zoho CRM's free edition is free for up to 3 users, with advanced reporting and mass email on paid plans. Neither is built for wholesaling, which isn't a criticism: they're built for a general small business, and you'll be adding the investor-specific parts yourself.

What seven jobs does a wholesaling CRM have to do?

Use this as your scorecard for any tool, free or paid:

  1. Capture every inquiry with its source. Web forms, calls, texts, list uploads. If the source isn't recorded at the door, you can't recover it later.
  2. Keep one record per seller and property. No duplicates when the same owner appears on three lists.
  3. Tell someone to act, fast. A new lead should create a task for a named person within minutes.
  4. Remember the next step. Every open lead has a follow-up date. Without it, a lead is just a name.
  5. Log calls and texts, and honor opt-outs. Including the seller who said stop on one number.
  6. Hold the deal. Offer, contract, dispo and closing on one record, with who did what.
  7. Report cost and result by source. Which campaign, list or caller produced contracts, not just leads.

Which limits bite wholesalers first?

Users

A solo operator needs one seat. A cold caller and a VA make three. If a tool allows two or three users on its free plan, you can hit the cap the week you hire. Check what the next tier costs for each extra person before it surprises you.

Records

A single skip-traced list can run into the thousands of rows. A free tier that caps contacts at 1,000 may be fine for a small team of buyers or sellers, but one list upload can fill it. Ask: how many sellers, properties and buyers will I have in a year?

Automation

The first automation most wholesalers need is "new lead means call task for the owner, due now", then "no contact in seven days means a reminder". If a free plan limits automated actions to one, or locks workflows to paid tiers, you're back to remembering.

Calls and texts

This is the one most people underestimate. If your dialer and texting tool don't write back to the CRM, you have two systems of record, and opt-outs live in one while follow-up runs from the other. Texting also has rules (carrier registration, consent, opt-outs) that a spreadsheet can't enforce. See texting sellers: 10DLC and consent.

Reporting

Free plans usually give you basic reports. The report wholesalers need is cost per contract by lead source, which needs your marketing spend, the source on every lead and the deal outcome in one place. That's rarely a free-tier feature, and it's what tells you where the next dollar should go. A cost per deal calculator can give you the numbers by hand while you're small.

Your data

The cheapest tool becomes the most expensive one if you can't get your data out in a usable form. Before you commit, export a test file and check that notes, activity history, dates and the source on every lead come with it.

What are the signs you've outgrown free?

  • A seller has been called twice by two different people this month.
  • You can't say which lead source produced last quarter's contracts without an hour of digging.
  • Follow-up depends on someone's calendar or memory, and leads go quiet when that person is busy.
  • You cap or avoid uploading a list because of the record limit.
  • A VA or cold caller needs access, and you can't limit what they see or export.
  • A seller who opted out got another text.
  • You're paying for three small tools that don't share data, and you spend time copying between them.

Two or three of these and the cost of staying free is already higher than the cost of moving. You can score your own setup in a few minutes with the Deal Leak Score and decide from there. The question "do I need a CRM at all?" has its own page: outgrown the spreadsheet?

How do you leave a free CRM without losing data?

  1. Export everything now, not on the day you cancel: contacts, deals, notes, tasks and any files.
  2. Write a field map. For each old field, name the new one, and note anything that has no home. The CRM switch field map is a starting point.
  3. Keep the old record's ID or a unique key in a field in the new system, so you can trace anything back.
  4. Move the open pipeline first, not the entire archive, and bring history in after.
  5. Run both for a short, planned overlap, with one clear rule about where new leads go.

Questions

Is there a free CRM for real estate wholesalers?

There are free tiers of general CRMs and there are spreadsheets, but I'm not aware of a free CRM built around wholesaling stages, dialer and texting. A free setup works for one person with low volume. It strains when a second person joins, when you upload large lists, or when you need follow-up automation and source reporting.

What is the best free CRM for real estate investors?

It depends on volume and people. A spreadsheet suits a solo investor proving a lead source. A free tier of a general CRM suits one to three people with simple follow-up. Check six limits first: users, records, automation, call and text logging, reporting by source, and data export.

Can I use HubSpot's free CRM for wholesaling?

You can, and it can work for a small team. As of the vendor page I checked in October 2026, the free CRM allows up to 2 users and 1,000 contacts with limited automation, and you'd build wholesaling stages, fields and reports yourself. Check HubSpot's current limits before you build on it.

Is Google Sheets enough as a wholesaling CRM?

For one person and a modest number of leads, yes. It has no reminders unless you build them, can't stop duplicate sellers, doesn't log calls or texts, and can't enforce opt-outs. The seller lead tracker on this site shows a setup that goes about as far as a sheet can.

What does a free CRM cost you in the end?

Your time and your leads. Time goes into building fields, stages and reports a wholesaling tool would include. Leads go missing when follow-up depends on memory. The cost shows up as slower response and no clear view of which marketing paid off.

Is there a cheap CRM for wholesalers?

Yes, several paid investor CRMs and general CRMs are inexpensive per user. Compare the total, including extra users, dialer minutes, skip tracing and texting, not just the seat price. The 11-option comparison on this site lays out what each includes.

When should a wholesaler move from a free CRM to Salesforce?

Usually around the point you have several people working leads, more than one lead source, and a process of your own that you want reported on, often a team of about 10 or more. Before that, an investor CRM or a free tier is often the better fit.
