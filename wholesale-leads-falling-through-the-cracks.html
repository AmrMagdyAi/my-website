How to stop leads falling through the cracks in a wholesaling business: one owner, one next step, one date

Almost every wholesaling team says the same thing: we are getting leads, and some of them just disappear. A seller said call me back in two weeks and nobody did. An offer went out and nobody followed up. A lead sat in a queue over a weekend. It feels like carelessness, but it is usually a systems problem. The lead had no owner, no next step, or no date, so nothing in the CRM could notice it was late. This guide shows the seven places leads leak, three rules that close them, how to make the CRM enforce the rules, and a one-hour check you can run on your own data today.

Short answer

Leads fall through the cracks when a record has no owner, no next step or no due date. Fix that with three rules: every open lead has one named owner, one next step and one date; nobody can save a lead without them; and a daily view shows every lead that is overdue or missing any of the three. Then check your own CRM for open leads with no owner, no next-step date or a date in the past. That count is the size of the leak.

Why do leads disappear even when everyone is working hard?

  • "Working on it" is not a state the CRM can see. A lead that is being handled and a lead that has been forgotten look the same until someone asks.
  • A conversation without a next step ends in silence. "Call me later" is a promise only if a date and a person are attached to it.
  • Handoffs drop things. Between a cold caller and an acquisitions rep, or between acquisitions and dispo, a lead changes hands and the follow-up belongs to nobody.
  • More leads make it worse, not better. Buying more leads into a system with no owners just adds to the pile nobody is watching. Fix the leak first.

What are the three rules?

  1. One owner. Every open lead has exactly one named person responsible, not a team and not a queue. A queue is where leads wait for an owner; once they have one it is that person's lead.
  2. One next step. Written so that someone else could do it: "call Ana about the roof estimate", not "follow up".
  3. One date. The date by which the next step happens. A lead with a date in the past is overdue, and overdue is a thing the CRM can show.

These are the same four things a stage needs in wholesaling pipeline stages: a rule to enter, a rule to leave, an owner and a time limit. The lead rules above are the version for a single record.

How do you make the CRM enforce the rules?

  • Required fields. Owner, next step and next-step date cannot be empty on an open lead; a validation rule blocks the save.
  • Default owner. Assign automatically on creation (round robin, by market or by source), so no lead starts unowned.
  • An overdue view per person. Every lead whose next-step date is today or earlier, sorted oldest first. This is the list each person starts the day with.
  • A "no next step" view for managers. Open leads missing an owner, a step or a date.
  • Stage age alerts. A lead that sits in one stage longer than its limit alerts the owner and then the manager.
  • Reply tasks. An inbound text, email or missed call creates a task for the owner with a due time.
  • A reason on every dead lead, so "gone" is a decision and not a drift.

What should the daily and weekly check look like?

Each morning, each person: clear the overdue view, then set a next step and date on every lead they touched yesterday.

Each Monday, the manager:

  • Count open leads with no owner, no next step or no date. The goal is zero.
  • Count overdue leads by owner. A person with a long list needs help, not a reminder.
  • Read the "not now" list for the coming week, so nobody who asked for a call back is forgotten.
  • Look at how many leads died this week, and why.

How do you find your own leaks in an hour?

  1. Export every open lead with owner, status, next step, next-step date, last activity and source.
  2. Count the ones with no owner, then no next step, then no date, then a date in the past. Four numbers.
  3. Sort by last activity. Leads untouched for longer than your own follow-up rhythm are the oldest leaks.
  4. Split by source and by person. If one source or one person holds most of the problem, the fix is specific.
  5. Fix the rules before the records. Add the required fields and the overdue view first, then work through the backlog.

Questions

How do I stop leads falling through the cracks in a wholesaling business?

Give every open lead one owner, one next step and one date, make the CRM refuse to save a lead without them, and give each person a daily view of overdue leads. Managers review leads with no owner, no step or no date every Monday.

Why do my wholesale leads keep getting lost?

Usually because they have no owner, no next step or no due date, so nothing in the system can tell they are late. Handoffs between callers, acquisitions and dispo, and 'call me later' conversations with no date attached, are the most common places.

What should I track to find leads that are slipping?

Open leads with no owner, no next-step date or a date in the past, the time since last activity, and which source and which person each problem lead belongs to.

Will a CRM stop leads falling through the cracks by itself?

No. A CRM only shows what you record. It helps once you make owner, next step and date required, assign leads on creation and build an overdue view; without those rules it is a place to store forgotten leads.

What do I do with a seller who says call me later?

Record the date they gave, the reason in their words, and a task for the owner on that date. A re-contact date is what separates a lead that is waiting from one that is lost.

Should I buy more leads if some are falling through?

Fix the leak first. More leads into a system with no owners and no dates adds to the pile nobody is watching. Count your open leads with no owner, no next step or no date, and bring that number to zero.
