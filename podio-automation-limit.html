Podio's new automation limit: what it means for a wholesaling team

A lot of wholesaling companies run on Podio, and most of the work in those setups is done by Podio Workflow Automation, the tool many teams still call Globiflow. Since September 1, 2026, that automation comes with a monthly allowance. If your Podio was built by a template vendor or a consultant a few years ago, you may not know how close you are to it. This page explains what changed, what counts, what happens when you hit the cap and how to decide what to do.

Short answer

Podio now includes 25,000 Workflow Automation actions a month. Extra capacity comes in prepaid, non-refundable add-ons, only on the Premium plan. There are no automatic overage charges: once you're over, automations above your capacity may be limited or fail until capacity frees up or you buy more. The flows that burn through the allowance are usually the ones that loop over many items or sync databases, not the ones that run once per new lead. Check your usage on the Workflow Automation Account Details page before you decide anything.

What changed with Podio pricing in 2026?

Podio, now owned by Progress Software, moved to what it calls hybrid pricing: you still pay for seats, and some usage now has limits with paid add-ons on top. The dates, from Podio's own FAQ:

DateWhat happened
July 1, 2026Customers notified, grace period started
August 1, 2026Usage add-ons went on sale
September 1, 2026Grace period ended and the new limits applied to existing customers

Two kinds of usage are covered:

  • Workflow Automation: the included base limit is now 25,000 actions a month. Add-ons are available only on the Premium plan.
  • API: the limits didn't change. It's 250 calls an hour for resource-intensive calls and 1,000 an hour for everything else, measured on a rolling hour. API add-ons are available on Plus and Premium. This is the one that matters if Zapier, Make or other tools talk to your Podio.

The add-on rules are worth reading twice, because they're stricter than most software:

  • Add-ons are prepaid, and Podio says to treat them as non-refundable.
  • They bill on the same monthly or annual cycle as your plan. Buying mid-cycle is prorated.
  • If you cancel, it takes effect at the end of the billing cycle.
  • Only the contract owner, the person on your account who handles billing, can buy them.

What counts as an action?

This is where teams get surprised. According to Podio:

  • A single action generally counts once each time it runs.
  • Actions that process several items may count once per item.
  • Loops may count based on the items and the number of times they repeat.
  • Database syncs count toward usage.
  • Some preparation or lookup steps may not count.

So a flow that runs once when a new lead comes in is cheap. A flow that runs every night and touches every open lead is not, because it can count once for every lead it touches, every night. Most wholesaling setups have a few of those: "days since last contact" updates, nightly status clean-ups, buyer list syncs, follow-up sweeps.

What happens when you hit the cap?

Podio's wording is that activity above your included or purchased capacity "may be limited or fail until capacity becomes available again", or until you buy an add-on. There's no automatic overage charge, so you won't get a surprise bill. You may get something worse for a wholesaler: automations that quietly don't run.

Think about what that means late in the month. A seller fills in your web form, the lead gets created, and the flow that assigns it to a rep, creates the callback task and sends the first text doesn't fire. Nobody notices until the seller has signed with someone faster.

Podio says alerts for contract owners and admins are planned. Until they arrive, someone on your team should look at usage every week, on the Workflow Automation Account Details page. API usage shows on the API Keys Settings page.

What are your options?

In the order I'd try them:

1. Measure

Look at last month's usage before you decide anything. If you're at 8,000 actions, you have time and this page is just a heads-up. If you're at 40,000, you need a plan this month. If someone else built your Podio, like a template vendor or a consultant, ask them for last month's number and which flows use the most.

2. Find the multipliers and redesign them

List every flow and mark the ones that run on a schedule, loop over items or sync data. For each one, ask whether it could run only when something changes instead of on everything every night. Archive dead leads so loops touch fewer items. Sync weekly instead of nightly where nobody needs fresh data. This is often enough on its own.

3. Buy add-ons

Fine for a short spike, or as a bridge while you redesign or move. Remember the rules: Premium only, prepaid, non-refundable, bought by the contract owner. If you find yourself buying them every month, that's a signal, not a fix.

4. Move some jobs out of Podio Workflow Automation

Some teams move heavy jobs to Make or Zapier. That doesn't make the work free: those tools connect through Podio's API, which has its own hourly limits, and they bill separately. Do it for specific jobs, not as a blanket fix.

5. Move off Podio

If your team has grown, your automations are what keep leads alive, and you can't get the reporting you need, this may be the push to move. For smaller teams, an all-in-one investor CRM can be a good landing spot. For teams of 10 or more who want their own system and real numbers, Salesforce is the option I build. If you move to Salesforce, are there limits there too?

Yes, and anyone who says otherwise is selling. Salesforce limits how much a single automation can do in one go, and it has daily allowances for scheduled jobs and API calls that grow with the number of licenses you have. What it doesn't have is a monthly action allowance for ordinary record-triggered automations that you top up in prepaid packs. The flows that create the callback task, send the alert and stop the follow-up when a seller replies run each time a record changes, without counting against a monthly meter.

The honest trade-off: Salesforce costs more to set up and needs someone to run it. It pays off when your team is big enough that missed leads and blind marketing spend cost more than the system. Questions

What is Podio's automation limit?

Since September 1, 2026, Podio Workflow Automation includes a base of 25,000 actions a month. Extra capacity is sold as add-ons, which are available only on the Premium plan.

When did the Podio usage limits start?

Podio notified customers and started a grace period on July 1, 2026, put add-ons on sale on August 1, 2026, and applied the new limits to existing customers on September 1, 2026.

What happens when we go over Podio's automation limit?

There are no automatic overage charges. Podio says activity above your included or purchased capacity may be limited or fail until capacity becomes available again or you buy an add-on. For a wholesaler, that can mean new leads that don't get assigned, tasked or texted.

Can we buy more Workflow Automation actions on Podio Plus?

No. Workflow Automation add-ons are available only on Podio Premium. API add-ons are available on Plus and Premium.

Are Podio add-ons refundable?

Podio says add-ons are prepaid and should be treated as non-refundable. They bill on the same cycle as your plan, mid-cycle purchases are prorated, and a cancellation takes effect at the end of the billing cycle.

Do Zapier and Make count against Podio's 25,000 actions?

Zapier and Make connect through Podio's API, which has its own limits that didn't change: 250 resource-intensive calls an hour and 1,000 other calls an hour, on a rolling hour. API add-ons are available on Plus and Premium.

How do I check my Podio automation usage?

Workflow Automation usage is on the Workflow Automation Account Details page, and API usage is on the API Keys Settings page. Podio says alerts for contract owners, admins and automation account owners are planned.

What uses up Podio automation actions fastest?

Flows that loop over many items, flows that process many items at once, and database syncs, because they can count once per item. A nightly flow that touches every open lead can use more actions in a month than all your new-lead automations combined.

Should we leave Podio because of the new limits?

Not automatically. If you're under the cap, or can get under it by redesigning scheduled flows, stay. If you're over every month, have 10 or more people and can't get the reporting you need, it's a good time to plan a move.
