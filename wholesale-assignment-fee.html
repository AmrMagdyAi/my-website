Wholesale assignment fee: set it from the buyer's margin.

An assignment fee is what a wholesaler is paid for assigning the purchase contract to a cash buyer. The fee that works is not a round number you pick in advance: it is whatever is left after the buyer's repairs, costs and required profit come out of the after-repair value, minus your contract price. Price it from the buyer's side and deals sell instead of sitting.

The max-fee formula

Buyer's costs = ARV × closing, holding and selling % Buyer's profit = the profit active buyers in that market need on this kind of deal Max fee = ARV − repairs − buyer's costs − buyer's profit − your contract price If max fee is below the fee you need, renegotiate the contract price or pass

Worked example

Made-up numbers to show the math. A house has a $250,000 after-repair value and needs $40,000 of repairs. The buyer's closing, holding and selling costs run about 10% of ARV, so $25,000. Buyers in that area want around 15% of ARV in profit on a medium rehab, so $37,500. Your contract price is $130,000. Max fee = $250,000 − $40,000 − $25,000 − $37,500 − $130,000 = $17,500. Ask $25,000 and the buyer's profit drops to $30,000; some will pass, and the ones who stay may try to renegotiate before closing.

Assign or double close?

An assignment is usually cheaper: one closing, and the fee is paid at that closing. A double close means you buy the property and resell it, often the same day, so you pay a second set of closing costs and may need transactional funding, but the buyer does not see your spread on the assignment paperwork. Questions

What is an assignment fee?

The amount a wholesaler is paid for assigning their purchase contract with the seller to an end buyer. The buyer pays the seller the contract price and pays the wholesaler the fee, usually at closing.

How much is a typical assignment fee?

There is no standard amount. It depends on the spread between your contract price and what buyers in that market will pay. The useful question is not what is typical but how much fee this deal can carry while the buyer still makes the profit they need.

Does the buyer see my fee?

With an assignment, usually yes: the fee appears in the assignment agreement and on the settlement statement. If a large fee would put the buyer or seller off, a double close is the common alternative, at the cost of a second set of closing costs.

Is assigning a contract legal everywhere?

Rules on wholesaling, assignment and disclosure vary by state and change over time, and some purchase contracts forbid assignment. For example, Oklahoma's SB 1075, in effect since November 1, 2025, requires written disclosures to the seller and a two-business-day right to cancel. This page is not legal advice; check the contract and ask a local real estate attorney before you assign.

What should my CRM track about fees?

The fee on every closed deal, the fee you planned when you went under contract, the lead source and the campaign. That shows which sources produce the deals with the biggest fees and how often fees get cut before closing.
