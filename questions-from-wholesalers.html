Do motivated sellers drop off in the last two or three months of the year?

Not in a way you should plan around without checking your own numbers. Seller motivation comes from situations like probate, vacancy, divorce, back taxes and repairs the owner can't afford, and those don't stop for the holidays. What often changes is contact: people are busier, travel more and pick up fewer unknown calls. Some investors also pause marketing at year end, which can mean less competition for the sellers who are ready. The honest answer for your market is in your own history, not in a group thread.

Do this in your CRM: Build one report of leads, appointments and signed contracts by month and by lead source, going back at least twelve months. Compare November and December with the rest of the year for each channel, then decide whether to cut, hold or shift spend.

I'm starting out with a full-time cold caller. What workflow should I set up first?

Set up the handoff before the dialing. A cold caller's job is to find interested sellers and pass them on cleanly, so the first thing to build is what happens after a yes: which details get captured (address, motivation, timeline, asking price, condition), who gets the lead, and how fast they call back. Then define a short list of call outcomes the caller picks on every call, such as no answer, wrong number, not interested, call back later and interested. Without those, you can't coach the caller and you can't tell a bad list from a bad pitch.

Do this in your CRM: Create a qualified lead stage with those details as required fields, and an automation that assigns each new qualified lead to you with a callback task due the same hour. Make call outcome a required field on every logged call.

How do you monitor cold callers’ metrics and calls when they use your dialer account?

Give every caller their own login, even on one dialer account. With a shared login, nobody can see who did what. With separate users, each call, recording and outcome is tied to one person, and you can compare callers working the same list. Track a few numbers per caller per day: dials, conversations with a real person, interested leads passed on, and the mix of call outcomes. Then listen to a handful of recordings each week, some picked at random and some marked interested. The numbers tell you where to look; the recordings tell you why.

Do this in your CRM: Sync dialer calls to the lead record with the caller as the owner, and build a daily dashboard grouped by user that shows dials, conversations, leads passed on and outcomes.

Which CRM should I use to build pipelines for my VAs: REsimpli, FreedomSoft, XLeads or something else?

The brand matters less than whether it does four things for your setup. One: separate logins and permissions, so each VA sees only the leads and stages they work. Two: pipeline stages you name yourself, with required fields before a lead moves forward. Three: your dialer and texting tools log calls and outcomes onto the lead automatically, not by copy and paste. Four: lead source stays on the record from first contact to closed deal, so you can report cost per deal by channel. Test any investor CRM against those four points during a trial, with a VA doing real work in it. Salesforce makes sense once you run several markets, teams or channels.

Do this in your CRM: Before you buy, write your stages on paper (new, contacted, qualified, appointment, offer, contract, closed, dead), rebuild them in the trial, and run five real leads through end to end.

I sent seven contracts last month and none got signed. What am I doing wrong?

Look at the system before the script. Three things usually explain a run of unsigned contracts. First, the offer: if each one is worked out differently, some will be too low for that seller's situation and others too high to work for you. Use one formula and write down the inputs. Second, follow-up: a sent contract with no scheduled next touch goes cold, and a seller talking to several investors often signs with whoever followed up. Third, reasons: if you don't record why each contract wasn't signed (price, timing, family, another buyer, no response), you'll keep guessing. After a month of recorded reasons, the pattern is usually clear.

Do this in your CRM: Add a contract sent stage that requires a follow-up task within 24 hours, and a required “reason not signed” picklist when a deal moves to dead.

My VA makes over 4,500 calls a week and gets no solid leads. What should I check?

Check four things, in this order. List quality: how old is the list, how was it skip traced, and how many numbers come back wrong or disconnected? If wrong numbers dominate, more dials won't help. Numbers tracked: dials alone say little; you need conversations, interested sellers and appointments to see where the drop happens. Call timing: compare contact rates by hour and day of week, because the hours that work vary by list and market. Call outcomes: if an outcome isn't logged on every call, you can't tell a bad list from a bad pitch. Then listen to a few recorded conversations.

Do this in your CRM: Make call outcome required, tag each lead with its list name and pull date, and run a weekly report of conversations and interested sellers by list and by hour of day.

What's the most consistent source of motivated seller leads?

The one that produces closed deals at the lowest cost in your market, and the only way to know which one that is, is to measure it. Ask a group and you'll hear cold calling, texting, direct mail, PPC and driving for dollars, and each answer is true for somebody. Comparing sources by cost per lead misleads, because cheap leads often convert worse. Compare cost per signed contract and cost per closed deal by source, over at least a quarter. For example, a channel with expensive leads can still be your cheapest per deal if more of its leads close.

Do this in your CRM: Make lead source a required field that stays on the record from first contact to closing, and report spend, leads, contracts and closed deals by source every month.

How do I calculate my offer price, or maximum allowable offer (MAO)?

Start with the common formula: MAO = after-repair value (ARV) × your rule percentage − repairs − your wholesale fee. Many buyers use 70%, but markets often use anything from 65% to 80% depending on price point and competition, so treat it as a dial, not a law. Example only: with an ARV of $200,000 at 70%, repairs of $30,000 and a $10,000 fee, MAO is $140,000 − $30,000 − $10,000 = $100,000. ARV should come from recent sold comps of similar renovated homes nearby, and repairs from a walkthrough plus a contingency. The formula is only as good as those two inputs.

Do this in your CRM: Put ARV, repairs, rule percentage and fee as fields on the deal, calculate MAO with a formula field, and require a review before any offer above MAO goes out.

How fast should I call back a missed seller call?

As fast as you can: within minutes if possible, and the same hour at the latest. A seller calling from your marketing may be calling other investors too, and usually talks to whoever answers first. The research on speed to lead isn't from real estate, but it points the same way. A Harvard Business Review study (2011) found firms that responded to a lead within an hour were about 7x more likely to qualify it. No comparable study exists yet for real estate investors, so measure your own time to callback and what it costs you.

Do this in your CRM: Have every missed call to a marketing number create a callback task on the lead, due in a few minutes and assigned to the next available rep, plus a daily report of missed calls and time to callback.

How do I know which marketing actually makes money?

Count only the deals you keep. Many reports count revenue when a contract is signed, but cancelled contracts and renegotiated fees never come back out, so some campaigns look profitable when they aren't. Kept-deal ROI compares what a campaign cost with the fees you actually received on deals that actually closed. To get there, lead source has to stay attached from first call to closing, and booked revenue and closed revenue have to be separate numbers. Review it every week with a short list of numbers rather than a crowded dashboard.

Do this in your CRM: Add separate booked fee and closed fee fields on the deal, a required fall-out reason, and a report of spend against closed fees by campaign. Check it every Monday.
