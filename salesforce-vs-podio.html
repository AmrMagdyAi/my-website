Salesforce vs Podio for wholesalers. Where each one wins in 2026

Podio has been the default first CRM for wholesalers for years: cheap, flexible and full of workspaces other investors built. Salesforce is what many of those teams look at once they have several reps, a second market or an owner who wants real numbers. This page compares the two honestly, with Podio's own published limits linked, and says when staying on Podio is the right call. I build on Salesforce, so weigh my view with that in mind.

Short version

Stay on Podio if you're a small team, your workspace works and you're under its automation allowance. Look at Salesforce when you have several reps or markets, need reports like cost per contract by source that you can't get from Podio, need per-field and per-record permissions, or keep running into Podio's limits, such as 25,000 Workflow Automation actions a month (since September 2026), 100 fields per app and 20,000 rows per Excel export. Salesforce costs more to set up and run, so a move only pays when one of those is costing you deals.

Where Podio wins

  • Cost to start. A small team can run Podio for a fraction of what Salesforce costs once you count licenses and setup.
  • Anyone can build in it. An owner or an ops person can add an app or a field in an afternoon, and a lot of wholesaling workspaces are available to copy.
  • Your team already knows it. Many wholesaling VAs and reps have worked in Podio before.
  • It's enough for a small, simple operation. One market, a few people and a workspace that works is a good reason to stay.

Where Salesforce wins

  • Structure that holds up as you grow. Leads, deals, properties, offers, buyers and assignments each have their own record and rules, so a second market or a rental side doesn't mean starting over.
  • Reports owners actually use. Cost per contract by source, days from offer to contract, offers over MAO by rep, all from the same data the team works in. See the Monday 5 for the ones I'd start with.
  • Permissions. VAs see only their queue, each market sees its own deals, and financial fields stay hidden from the people who don't need them. See Salesforce access for VAs and cold callers.
  • Automation without a monthly meter. Flow has limits per transaction and daily allowances for scheduled jobs that grow with your licenses, but no monthly count of actions to top up. The record-triggered automations that create tasks and stop cadences run each time a record changes, without counting against a monthly meter.
  • History. Opportunity stage changes are tracked out of the box, and field history tracking covers up to 20 fields per object, so you can see who changed what and when.

The cost question, honestly

Salesforce is not the cheap option. You pay for licenses, setup and someone to look after it, plus the dialer, texting and data tools you connect. Podio's per-seat price is low and public.

The move pays when something you can't do in Podio is costing you deals: leads that slip because an automation hit its cap, marketing money spent without knowing cost per contract by source, or a second market you can't keep separate. If none of that is true, stay. To see which Salesforce edition you'd be quoted for, use the edition picker.

What does moving from Podio involve?

Mostly data decisions: what history you keep, how lead source carries through to every closed deal, and what Podio's Excel export leaves out (files, comments, tasks and activity aren't in it). Run a 50-record test before you move the rest, and keep Podio read-only for a while after. The details are in two guides:

Questions

Is Salesforce better than Podio for wholesalers?

For a small team with a simple process, Podio is usually enough and much cheaper. Salesforce is better when you have several reps or markets, need reports like cost per contract by source, need field- and record-level permissions, or keep hitting Podio's limits.

What are Podio's limits?

Podio's documentation lists 100 fields per app, 500,000 items per app, 20,000 rows per Excel import or export, 50 reports per app and API limits of 250 resource-intensive and 1,000 other calls an hour. Since September 1, 2026, Workflow Automation includes 25,000 actions a month.

Who owns Podio?

Podio is owned by Progress Software, which publishes its documentation and the 2026 hybrid pricing changes.

Can Salesforce do what Globiflow does?

Yes. Salesforce Flow handles the same kinds of jobs as Podio Workflow Automation, the tool formerly called Globiflow: creating tasks, updating records, sending texts through a connected app and running on a schedule, without a monthly action allowance.

Is Podio cheaper than Salesforce?

Yes, almost always, especially for small teams. Salesforce adds licenses, setup and ongoing admin. It's worth it only when something Podio can't do is costing you deals.

How long does it take to move from Podio to Salesforce?

It depends on the number of apps, records and integrations. The slow part is usually deciding what to keep and cleaning duplicates. The switch guide on this site lays out a 30-60-90 day plan.

Can I run Podio and Salesforce together?

For a short time during a move, yes, so no lead is lost. Long term, two CRMs means two versions of the truth, so pick one as the system of record.
