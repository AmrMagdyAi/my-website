Do I need a CRM yet? Spreadsheet vs CRM for wholesalers.

You need a CRM when a lead can be forgotten without anyone noticing. Deal count matters less than people, follow-up and lead source. If one person works every lead and checks the sheet daily, a spreadsheet can carry you. Once two people touch leads, follow-ups run past a week, or you can't say which list paid for your last deal, the sheet starts costing you deals.

Seven signs you've outgrown the spreadsheet

Check the ones that are true

☐ More than one person calls or texts sellers
☐ Follow-ups depend on someone remembering
☐ You can't say which list or campaign produced your last closed deal
☐ A missed call doesn't turn into a callback task by itself
☐ Leads with "not right now" have no next date
☐ Your buyers list is a text thread or a column of phone numbers
☐ Monday numbers take an hour to rebuild by hand

Two or more checked: the spreadsheet is already costing you. Wholesalers describe it the same way on BiggerPockets: "leads die in the follow-up".

What a spreadsheet still does well

Keep it if

You're solo, every lead is touched daily, and you're still testing which marketing works. A sheet with lead source, status, next follow-up date and notes is a fine start. Make next follow-up date a column you sort by every morning.

The cheapest next step

1. Write your stages from new lead to closed on one page 2. Make lead source, next follow-up date and status required on every lead 3. Move the sheet into a free or low-cost CRM trial and test it for 2 weeks 4. Turn on two automations: missed call = callback task, no next date = alert 5. Only then compare paid tools against the flow you wrote in step 1

Looking at low-cost options? A recent BiggerPockets thread compares them: low to no cost wholesaling CRMs. Whatever you pick, check it against the wholesaling CRM checklist.

Questions

How many leads before I need a CRM?

There is no single number. The trigger is people and follow-up: once two people touch leads, or follow-ups depend on memory, a CRM pays for itself by keeping leads from going quiet.

Can I use Google Sheets as a CRM?

Yes at the start, if one person works every lead daily and the sheet has lead source, status, next follow-up date and notes. It breaks when you add people, texting and automated reminders.

What should I set up first in a new CRM?

Required lead source, a next follow-up date on every open lead, a missed call creating a callback task, and a short list of stages. Fancy dashboards can wait.

Is Salesforce too much for a new wholesaler?

Usually yes. Start with a spreadsheet or an investor CRM. Salesforce makes sense once you have several people, markets or reporting needs your tool cannot handle.
