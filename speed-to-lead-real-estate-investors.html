Speed to lead for real estate investors: the first-hour rule.

Speed to lead is the time between a seller reaching out and a real person on your team talking to them. For investors and wholesalers it decides who gets the deal: motivated sellers usually contact several buyers, and they often go with the first one who answers and sounds able to help. Aim to reach every inbound lead within the hour, and measure it.

What the research says

The best-known study is The Short Life of Online Sales Leads (Harvard Business Review, March 2011) by James Oldroyd, Kristina McElheran and David Elkington. Looking at 1.25 million leads across 42 US companies, firms that contacted a lead within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify it as firms that waited longer, and more than 60 times as likely as firms that waited 24 hours or more. In a separate audit of 2,241 US companies, only 37% responded within an hour and 23% never responded.

That study covered web leads in general, not seller calls. A missed seller call is the same problem in a sharper form: the seller already reached out, and the clock is running against every other investor they called.

How to measure your speed to lead

Lead created time = when the call, form or text came in First conversation time = when a person on your team actually spoke with the seller Speed to lead (minutes) = first conversation time − lead created time Report: median by lead source, by hour of day, and by rep, every Monday

Use the median, because one lead that waited three days will distort an average. Split by hour of day: most teams find their gap is evenings and weekends, which is when many sellers call.

Five rules that fix it in any CRM

1. Every missed call becomes a task

When a call to a marketing number is not answered, the CRM creates the lead if it does not exist and a callback task due in minutes. No missed call should exist only in the phone system.

2. Route to whoever is free

New leads and missed-call tasks go to the next available rep, not to the person who owns the number or the market. A lead waiting for one busy rep is a slow lead.

3. Escalate after 15 minutes

If nobody has touched a new lead or a callback task in 15 minutes, it moves to a second person or a manager. Pick the number that fits your team, then enforce it.

4. Acknowledge after hours, call first thing

Outside business hours, send the seller a short text that a person will call at a set time, then make that call the first task of the morning. Track the acknowledgment and the conversation as separate times.

5. Put the number on the Monday report

Median speed to lead and missed calls not returned, by source. What gets reviewed every week gets faster.

Questions

What is speed to lead?

The time between a seller reaching out (a call, a web form, a text reply) and a real person on your team having a conversation with them. Measure it to the first conversation, not to the first automated text.

What is a good speed to lead for a wholesaler or home-buying company?

Under an hour is the bar the research supports, and in practice the goal is minutes for inbound calls and web forms during business hours. More important than a target is knowing your real number by lead source and by hour of day.

Does an automatic text count as a response?

It helps, because the seller knows they reached someone. But it is not a conversation. Track automated acknowledgment and first human conversation as two separate times.

How do I measure speed to lead if my CRM does not track it?

Add two date-time fields to the lead: when the lead came in and when the first real conversation happened. A formula field gives the minutes between them. Report the median, not the average, by source and by hour.

Why do missed calls matter so much?

A missed inbound call is a seller who already chose to call you. If nobody calls back fast, they talk to the next investor on their list, and the lead never shows up in your CRM at all, so your reports cannot see the loss.
