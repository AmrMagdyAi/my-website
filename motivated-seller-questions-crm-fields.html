Motivated seller questions: what to ask, and where each answer goes in the CRM

Most seller-question lists stop at the questions. The part that decides whether a call becomes an offer is what happens to the answer: if it lands in a notes box, the next person starts again, the lead can't be scored, and nobody can report on why sellers sell. This page pairs each question with the CRM field it should fill, why that field matters later, and a plain way to ask it. The questions are the kind a cold caller or an acquisitions rep asks in a first conversation. They're written for a seller who may be having a hard time, so they ask rather than push.

Short answer

Ask in five groups: situation (why they're selling and by when), property (condition and who lives there), money (what they'd accept and what's owed), people (who owns it and who decides) and process (other offers and how to reach them). Give each answer its own field, a picklist where you'll count it, so leads can be scored, the handoff is complete and the offer is based on facts. Ask for consent to text on the same call, and record it with the date.

How should you ask?

  • Listen first. Ask one open question, then let the seller talk. The answers to later questions often come up without asking.
  • Don't interrogate. Five or six well-placed questions beat twenty. Record what you learned, not what you asked.
  • Be honest about the purpose. You're trying to see whether you can make an offer that works for them. Sellers in hardship notice pressure.
  • Write down what they said in the field right away, in their words where it helps ("needs to move before school starts").

How do you make them fields in Salesforce?

  • Picklists for anything you'll count (reason, timeline, condition, occupancy), with a short list you've defined; see tags, fields or campaigns.
  • Currency fields for asking price and mortgage balance, marked as approximate.
  • Required fields tied to the stage: not everything on first contact. The handoff needs reason, timeline, condition, price and who decides; see the handoff checklist.
  • A screen flow that walks through the questions in order, so the caller fills the fields while talking instead of afterwards.
  • Page layout that shows the answered and unanswered fields at a glance, so the next person asks only what's missing.

What should you do with the answers?

  1. Score the lead from reason, timeline and equity, and call the highest first.
  2. Route it: a probate or foreclosure lead may go to a specific person.
  3. Set the follow-up cadence from the timeline and the preferred contact method (cadence builder).
  4. Fill the offer worksheet from property and money fields, not from memory.
  5. Report on reasons: which situations produce contracts, and which sources bring those sellers.

Questions

What questions should I ask a motivated seller?

Ask in five groups: why and by when they're selling, what the property is like, what they'd accept and what's owed, who owns it and who decides, and whether there are other offers and how to reach them. Record each answer in its own field, not in notes.

What information do I need before making an offer on a wholesale deal?

Reason and timeline, condition and occupancy, asking price, mortgage balance and any liens, every person on title and other decision makers, and the agreed next step. These are the facts the handoff and the offer worksheet depend on.

How do I qualify a seller lead without sounding pushy?

Ask one open question and listen, ask five or six questions rather than twenty, and explain you're trying to see whether you can make an offer that works for them. Let the answers fill fields afterwards.

Which seller answers should be required fields in a CRM?

Require reason for selling, timeline, condition, price expectation, who else decides and the agreed next step before a lead is handed to acquisitions, and require consent and its date before texting. Don't require all of it on the first touch.

How do I find out who else decides on the sale?

Ask who is on the title and whether anyone else is involved, such as a spouse, sibling, heir or attorney, and invite them to the appointment. It's the most common reason offers stall, so give it its own field.

Where should I record whether a seller agreed to texts?

In a consent checkbox with a date and the source of the consent, separate from notes. Make every tool that texts read it, and copy opt-outs to every duplicate record.
