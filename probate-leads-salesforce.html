Probate deals run on the court's clock. Tracking probate leads in Salesforce

Probate is one of the steadiest lead sources in investing, and one of the easiest to work badly. The person you need to reach is grieving, the decision may belong to someone other than the person you're talking to, and the court sets the pace. A CRM built for "call fast and close in two weeks" loses these deals. This page is for owners and acquisitions managers who work probate leads, or want to, and need Salesforce to keep track of a deal that can take most of a year.

Short answer

Build a probate lead around the whole estate rather than the house alone: the person who died, the personal representative who can sell, the attorney, the heirs, and the case with its county, case number, filing date and the date letters were issued. Track the dates that decide when a sale can happen, follow up on a slow, respectful cadence that can run for months, and keep probate deals in their own pipeline so your usual "no activity in seven days" alarms don't fire on them. Rules vary by state, and none of this is legal advice.

Why are probate leads different?

  • The owner isn't the one deciding. The person who owned the house has died. The personal representative, called the executor when a will names one and the administrator when the court appoints one, acts for the estate once the court issues letters testamentary or letters of administration.
  • The court sets the pace. Depending on the state, probate typically takes six to twelve months, faster for simple estates and a year or more for complex ones.
  • A sale may need the court's OK. In many states the executor needs court approval before selling real estate. Some require the court to confirm the sale, which can mean a hearing where other buyers can bid more. Some wills give the executor authority to sell without approval.
  • Families are involved. Heirs in other states, siblings who disagree, someone living in the house. Patience and a record of every conversation matter more here than anywhere else.
  • The filings are public. Probate records usually include the will, the petition, the inventory and the representative's name, sometimes with contact details. That's where most probate lead lists come from.

How do you set it up in Salesforce?

  • Lead source "Probate", with each list tracked as its own campaign, such as one county and month, so you know which lists pay.
  • A deal for each property once the lead is qualified, with the representative as the main contact and the attorney and heirs linked by role.
  • A probate case record if you work more than a handful: case number, county, filing date, letters date, decedent, representative and attorney, linked to every property in the estate. One estate can hold several properties, and a case record means you enter those details once.
  • Its own pipeline. A separate record type or sales process for probate, with stages that match the case: researching, letter sent, talking with the representative, waiting on letters, offer made, waiting on court approval, under contract.
  • Duplicate checks on case number and property address. The same estate often shows up on a probate list, a vacant list and a tax list. See duplicate sellers in Salesforce.

What timeline are you working with?

A typical sequence for an estate with a will, from LegalZoom's guides. Every state differs, so treat the durations as rough.

StepTypical timingWhat it means for you
Petition filed with the courtOften within 30 to 60 days of the deathThe case appears in public records
Hearing, appointment and letters issuedAbout 2 to 8 weeks after filingThe representative can now act for the estate
Notice to creditors and claim periodUsually 3 to 6 monthsDebts get sorted, often the longest step
Inventory and appraisalAbout 30 to 90 daysThe estate learns what the house is worth
Sale, with court approval or confirmation where requiredA confirmation hearing is often 2 to 4 weeks after an offer is acceptedYour offer may face higher bids at the hearing

LegalZoom puts the whole run, from filing to closing a probate sale, at typically six to twelve months, with simple estates closing in three or four and complex ones taking eighteen months or more.

That timeline breaks most investor CRMs. A deal that sits in one stage for four months isn't stale; it's waiting on a court. Keep probate out of your normal aging alarms and give it alarms of its own, built on the case dates above.

What follow-up fits probate?

  • Start slow and in writing. I'd lead with a letter to the representative rather than a call, and give the family time first. Whatever your rule is, put it in Salesforce so every rep follows it, such as no first contact until a set number of weeks after the date of death.
  • Let the case dates drive the tasks. A check-in a week after letters are issued, another before the creditor deadline, another after the inventory. Scheduled paths in a Flow can create those tasks from the dates, and they move if the dates change.
  • Space calls weeks apart, not days. A representative handling an estate gets a lot of calls from investors. Being the patient, organized one helps.
  • Bring in the attorney when the representative is comfortable with it. Attorneys often know when the estate will be ready to sell.
  • Write everything down. A probate deal can pass between reps over the months it takes. The next person needs to know who said what, and who doesn't want calls.
  • Opt-outs still apply. The consent and do-not-call rules in reactivating dead seller leads apply here too.

How do you get probate data into Salesforce?

Probate filings are public, and anyone can usually see the case file through the local court, online in some counties and in person in others. Most teams buy lists from a data vendor or pull them from county records. Either way:

  1. Import with the case details: case number, county, filing date, decedent, representative's name and mailing address, and the property address.
  2. Match duplicates on the way in, by case number and property address, before anyone sends a letter.
  3. Tag each list as a campaign, one per county and month, so cost per contract by list is a report, not a guess.
  4. Find phone numbers for the representative, not the decedent, if your process includes calls.

Which reports matter?

  1. Probate pipeline by stage and case milestone: letters issued or not, creditor period over or not.
  2. Case dates coming up in the next 30 days, so every milestone gets a call.
  3. Cost per contract by probate list, by county and month.
  4. Letter to conversation to contract, to see where probate deals drop out.
  5. Probate deals by age, judged against your own history rather than your two-week wholesale deals.

Questions

How do you track probate leads in a CRM?

Link each property to the estate: the decedent, the personal representative, the attorney, the heirs and the case details, including county, case number, filing date and letters date. Keep probate deals in their own pipeline with follow-up tied to the case dates.

Who can sell a house in probate?

The personal representative, called the executor when named in a will or the administrator when appointed by the court, once the court issues letters testamentary or letters of administration. Many states also require court approval or confirmation of the sale.

How long does a probate sale take?

LegalZoom puts typical probate real estate sales at six to twelve months from the probate filing to closing, with simple estates sometimes closing in three or four months and complex ones taking eighteen months or more.

Are probate records public?

Generally yes. Once a case is filed, the will, petition and inventory are usually part of the public record, available through the local probate court, and some counties put them online.

Should probate leads have their own pipeline in Salesforce?

Yes. Probate deals wait on the court for months, so they trip normal aging alarms and need different stages, dates and follow-up. A separate record type or sales process keeps them visible without false alarms.

When should you first contact a probate lead?

There's no rule that fits every family. I'd start with a letter to the personal representative rather than a call and give the family time. Whatever you decide, set it as a rule in Salesforce so every rep follows it.

How do you handle several heirs on one property?

Link each heir to the deal with a contact role, track whether they agree, and keep the personal representative as the main contact, since they act for the estate.
