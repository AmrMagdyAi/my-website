Lead response time (speed to lead)

Answering fast is the single most studied lever in lead conversion. The research is old, mostly outside real estate, and still consistent: minutes matter, and most companies are slow.

8 statistics everyone quotes that I couldn't verify

These appear on hundreds of blogs and sales decks. I traced each one and could not find a primary source, or found it was misquoted. Don't repeat them as fact.

“78% of customers buy from the company that responds first.”

Usually credited to a “Lead Connect survey” with no title, year, sample or link. No primary source found. Closest real data: in 6sense’s 2025 B2B survey, buyers chose the vendor they first contacted about 80% of the time.

“35–50% of sales go to the vendor that responds first.”

Credited to InsideSales.com, but it is not in their 2007, 2014 or 2021 studies.

“80% of sales need 5 follow-ups; 2% close on first contact.”

Credited to a “National Sales Executive Association” that VentureBeat found does not exist. Use the Velocify and InsideSales follow-up data above instead.

“44% of salespeople give up after one follow-up.”

No primary source found. Real data: half of leads were never called a second time (Velocify 2012).

“85% of callers who reach voicemail won’t call back.”

Repeated widely with no original survey. Real data: 26% of calls unanswered and only 2% of those callers left a voicemail (Invoca 2021).

“The MIT study: respond in 5 minutes and you’re 100x more likely to close.”

The 100x is the odds of contacting the lead, not closing. And it is InsideSales.com data analyzed by an MIT professor, not an MIT publication.

“The average lead response time is 47 hours.”

A misquote of HBR’s 42 hours.

“Investors buy 1 in 3 homes.”

Out of date. BatchData puts investors at 26.7% of purchases in Q2 2026; Redfin puts it at 19% in Q1 2026 using a different method. Quote one with its source.

What this means for a wholesaling team

1. Measure your own speed to lead

Every study above says minutes matter, and most companies don't know their number. Stamp the time a lead arrives and the time someone actually talks to the seller, and report the gap weekly. The speed to lead guide shows how.

2. Make the sixth attempt automatic

93% of converted leads were reached by the sixth call attempt, yet most leads get one or two. Let the CRM schedule the attempts so persistence doesn't depend on memory. Copy a follow-up cadence.

3. Treat a missed call as a lead, not a voicemail

Only 2% of unanswered callers leave a voicemail. Turn every missed call into a callback task and a text within minutes. See what yours cost with the missed call calculator.

4. Fix the data before buying more leads

76% of CRM users say less than half their data is accurate. Required lead source, next follow-up date and dead reason fix most of it. Use the CRM field kit.

5. Margins are thinner, so every lead counts

Flip ROI fell to 25.5% in 2025, the lowest since 2008, and investor purchases are down. Teams that keep more of the leads they already pay for will outlast teams that just buy more.

How these were checked

Every statistic was read on the linked page in October 2026. Where the original was paywalled or blocked (the HBR article and the Velocify study), I used a contemporaneous report of it and say so. Vendor research (InsideSales, Velocify, Invoca, CallRail, Validity, HubSpot, Carrot, BatchData) is labeled by publisher so you can weigh it. Most lead-response research is not specific to real estate. Spotted an error or a newer source? Email a correction.
