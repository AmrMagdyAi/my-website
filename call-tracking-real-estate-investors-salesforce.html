Call tracking for real estate investors: every seller call tied to the campaign that rang the phone

A motivated seller usually picks up the phone. That is good for you and bad for your numbers: a web form tells you where a lead came from, and a phone call tells you nothing unless you set something up. The seller says "I got a letter" or "I saw your sign", the rep types "mailer" into the notes, and three months later you can't say which mailer wave paid for itself. Call tracking fixes it by giving each source its own phone number, or a number that changes by visitor, and writing the source to the seller's record. This guide explains the two kinds of tracking number, which one fits each channel, what to log in Salesforce and what goes wrong.

Short answer

Use a dedicated tracking number for each offline source (each mailer wave, sign, billboard, Google Business Profile listing) and a pool of numbers swapped in by script for website visitors. Route all of them to your real line, and log the source, campaign, caller number, duration, recording link and whether the caller is new or returning on the seller's record in Salesforce. Match callers to existing sellers by phone before creating a record, and keep every printed number live for months.

Why are phone calls your biggest blind spot?

Marketing for sellers is full of channels that end in a call: direct mail, yard and bandit signs, cold-call callbacks, text blasts, a Google Business Profile listing, a pay-per-click ad with a call button. A form submission carries the page and the campaign with it. A call arrives as a phone number and a voice. Without tracking, the source of every call comes from what the seller remembers and what a rep types, which are both unreliable. The cost shows up in your attribution: calls are often the best leads, and they end up filed as "unknown" or "referral". You then cut the channel that was working or fund the one that only looked good on forms. The attribution rules for what to do with the data are in which campaign gets credit.

What are the two kinds of tracking number?

These are the two types call-tracking vendors offer; I'll use CallRail's definitions as one example, and other vendors work the same way.

  • Source tracking number: a single, static number that tracks one source, such as one mailer wave, a billboard or a Google ad extension. Every call to it is that source.
  • Website pool with dynamic number insertion (DNI): a set of numbers that a small script swaps onto your web pages in place of your normal number, based on how each visitor arrived. It stores the source in a cookie so the same visitor sees the same number on return visits. A pool captures visitor-level detail such as pages visited and paid keywords, which source numbers can't.

One practical caveat on DNI: the script looks for your phone number on the page, so the number must be visible in the page's HTML or an image in a normal format such as (111) 222-3333.

What should each call write to Salesforce?

  • Source and campaign from the number dialed, mapped to a Salesforce campaign. This is what makes cost per contract a normal report.
  • Caller's phone number, in the cleaned format your duplicate rule matches on.
  • New or returning caller. A call from a known seller should reopen their record and notify the owner, not create a new lead (see duplicate seller leads).
  • Call duration, direction and a recording link, for coaching and for settling "I never got a call back" arguments.
  • For web pool calls, the keyword or landing page, when your vendor provides it.
  • Missed or unanswered flag, which should create a callback task straight away; see missed-call alerts.

How data reaches Salesforce depends on the vendor. CallRail's page for its Salesforce integration says calls are tied to campaigns and recordings can be listened to in Salesforce, and lists a Salesforce Enterprise or Unlimited plan as a requirement. If your edition doesn't qualify, a Zapier or webhook connection that writes the same fields can do the job; see Salesforce integrations and Zapier with Salesforce. Check the vendor's current requirements before you buy.

What is the setup, in order?

  1. List your channels and decide how finely you want to split them. One number per mailer wave is plenty; one per envelope color is not.
  2. Buy and label numbers in a naming scheme that sorts (channel, market, month).
  3. Create a Salesforce campaign for each and store the number on it, so incoming calls can look up their campaign.
  4. Route every number to your real line and play a short recording notice if you record (see the note on recording below).
  5. Install the website script and check that the number swap works on every page that shows a phone number.
  6. Connect to Salesforce, test with five calls from different phones, and check that each lands on the right record with the right campaign.
  7. Add the numbers to your SOPs so no one prints a mailer without a tracked number; see SOPs inside your CRM.

What goes wrong?

  • Releasing a number too soon. A seller may keep a letter for months. Keep printed numbers live until you're sure the campaign is dead, and forward them to your real line.
  • Too many numbers. Split sources only as far as you will report on them.
  • Tracking the first touch and ignoring the rest. A seller can see a sign, get a letter and call after a text. Decide your credit rule before you read the report.
  • Creating a lead for every call. Repeat callers and wrong numbers inflate counts; match on phone first.
  • Recording laws. States differ on consent to record calls, and several require all parties to agree. Add a recording notice at the start of tracked calls, and check the rules for the states you operate in. This is information, not legal advice.
  • Treating the call as the lead's start. If the call went unanswered, the clock for speed to lead began when it rang.

What reports should call tracking give you?

  1. Calls by source and market, weekly, with answered and missed split.
  2. Calls to appointments to contracts by source. The reason to track at all.
  3. Cost per call and cost per contract by campaign, using the spend on each campaign; see the cost per deal calculator.
  4. Missed calls by hour, which tells you when to staff the phone.
  5. Share of "direct" or "unknown" calls. If it creeps up, a channel is missing a number.

Questions

How do I track which marketing is making my phone ring?

Give each offline source its own tracking number (each mailer wave, sign type, Google Business Profile listing) and use a dynamic number pool on your website. Route all numbers to your real line and write the source and campaign to the seller's record, so you can report calls, appointments and contracts by source.

What is dynamic number insertion?

A script on your website that swaps your normal phone number for a tracking number based on how the visitor arrived, and remembers the source in a cookie. It captures details such as pages visited and paid keywords. The number must be visible on the page in a standard format for the swap to work.

Do I need a different phone number for each mailer?

One per mailer wave or list is enough to learn which mailing pays. Don't split finer than you'll report on, and keep each printed number live for months because sellers keep letters.

Can call tracking connect to Salesforce?

Yes. CallRail, for example, has a Salesforce integration that ties calls to campaigns and lets you hear recordings in Salesforce; its page lists a Salesforce Enterprise or Unlimited plan as a requirement. Other vendors differ, and Zapier or a webhook can write the same fields on other editions. Check the vendor's current requirements.

Is it legal to record calls from sellers?

It depends on the state. Several states require all parties to consent to recording, so add a recording notice at the start of tracked calls and check the rules where you operate. This is information, not legal advice.

How do I stop duplicate leads from repeat callers?

Match the caller's cleaned phone number against existing sellers before creating a record, reopen the existing record and notify its owner, and keep the original source and the new one. See the duplicate seller guide.
