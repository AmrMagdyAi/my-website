Moving off Podio: a migration checklist for wholesalers.

Leaving Podio is mostly a data decision, not a software one. Decide what history you keep, carry lead source through to every closed deal, plan for what Podio's Excel export leaves out, and test with 50 records before you move the rest. The checklist below covers it in ten steps.

Signs you've outgrown your current setup

Check the ones that are true

☐ Monday numbers get rebuilt in a spreadsheet by hand
☐ Closed deals don't show which campaign produced them
☐ Missed seller calls don't become callback tasks on their own
☐ The person who built the workspace has left, and nobody wants to touch it
☐ A second market or a bigger team is coming

Two or more checked usually means it's time to rebuild or move. Score the whole funnel in two minutes with the free Deal Leak Score.

Three options

1. Rebuild in Podio

Cheapest when the team knows Podio and the problems are messy fields and missing automations. Rebuild against the wholesaling CRM checklist.

2. Move to an investor CRM

Built for wholesaling and quick to start. Check lead source carried to the closed deal and fall-out reasons before you sign.

3. Move to Salesforce

More setup, but it fits teams with their own process, several markets or custom reporting. I build on Salesforce, so weigh that.

The 10-step migration checklist

1. List every app, field and automation in the workspace 2. Decide what moves: open leads, active deals, buyers, closed deals with source 3. Map each field to the new system; fix picklists and drop unused fields 4. Keep lead source, created date and closed date on every record 5. Export each app (Excel export, filtered batches above 20,000 items) 6. Plan separately for comments, files and tasks (not in the export) 7. Merge duplicate sellers by phone and email 8. Import 50 records, check them with the team, fix the mapping 9. Move the rest, reconnect phone, text and web forms, then cut over 10. Keep Podio read-only for 30 days; compare Monday numbers in both

Export details from Podio's help page on exporting to Excel: an export from the first All items view includes all fields plus created on, created by and tags; images, files, tasks, comments and activity are not included; each export is capped at 20,000 items.

Questions

What does a Podio Excel export include?

Per the Podio help page, an export from the first All items view includes all app fields plus metadata such as created on, created by and tags. Images, files, tasks, comments and activity are not included, and each export is capped at 20,000 items, so larger apps are exported in filtered batches.

How do I keep comments and call notes when I leave Podio?

They are not in the Excel export, so plan for them separately: decide which notes matter (often only open deals and active sellers), and move those through the Podio API or by hand. Archive the rest as a read-only export.

How long does a migration take?

It depends on the number of apps, records and integrations. The slow parts are usually deciding what to keep and cleaning duplicates, not the import itself. Run a 50-record test before the full move.

Should I rebuild in Podio instead?

Sometimes yes. If the team knows Podio and the problems are messy fields and missing automations, a clean rebuild against the checklist can be the cheapest fix.

Can you migrate Podio to Salesforce?

Yes, that is part of my work. I build Salesforce for real estate investors and wholesalers, so weigh my view with that in mind.
