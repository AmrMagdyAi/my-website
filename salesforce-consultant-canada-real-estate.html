Salesforce for Canadian real estate investors: the same deal system, set up for Canadian rules

I build Salesforce for home-buying companies and wholesalers. If you run an investing business in Canada, two questions matter before anything else: does the same system work there, and what has to change? This page answers both, for owners of home-buying, wholesaling and BRRRR businesses. I work remotely, and Eastern and Central business hours fall inside my working day.

Short answer

The core system is the same: every seller lead answered fast, offers on the deal record, a pipeline to closing, and ROI counted on deals that actually closed and properties you keep. What changes in Canada is mostly consent. Calls fall under the CRTC's telemarketing rules and the National Do Not Call List, and texts and emails under CASL, so the CRM should record consent, do-not-call status and unsubscribes for every person. Set CAD as the currency, put the province on every lead and deal, and get each province's assignment and wholesaling rules from a local lawyer.

What stays the same as a US home-buying team?

The deal flow doesn't change at the border. A Canadian team still needs:

  • Every seller lead captured with its source. Calls and forms create leads on their own, and every missed call becomes a callback task. See missed-call alerts in Salesforce.
  • Offers made from the same numbers. After-repair value, repairs and maximum offer on the deal record, not in separate spreadsheets. The MAO calculator shows the math.
  • A pipeline from first call to closing, with a board for every deal under contract. See transaction coordination in Salesforce.
  • Buyers stored with the areas and property types they buy, so a new deal goes to the right people first. See dispositions in Salesforce.
  • ROI by campaign on deals that actually closed, with one consistent rule for which campaign gets credit. See lead attribution for investors.

Cold calling: the CRTC rules and the National Do Not Call List

If your team calls homeowners, plan for the CRTC's Unsolicited Telecommunications Rules to apply, and confirm with a lawyer how they apply to your calls. According to the CRTC:

  • Telemarketers must register with the National Do Not Call List (DNCL), even for exempt calls. Anyone making calls that aren't exempt must also subscribe to the list while calling, and must not call numbers on it.
  • Calls are allowed from 9:00 a.m. to 9:30 p.m. on weekdays, and from 10:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. on weekends.
  • Telemarketers must keep their own do-not-call list, add a request within 14 days, and keep the number on it for 3 years and 14 days.
  • Callers must clearly identify themselves.

What that means in the CRM:

  • Every imported list is screened before it's loaded, and each phone number carries the date it was checked and the result.
  • An internal do-not-call flag follows the person across every record, including duplicates. See duplicate sellers in Salesforce.
  • Your dialer's calling windows follow the seller's province and time zone.
  • A report of calls to flagged numbers, which should always be empty.

Texts and emails: what CASL means for your CRM

Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) covers commercial electronic messages: messages where one of the purposes is to get the recipient to do business with you. That includes email, texts and direct messages on social platforms. According to the CRTC's CASL guidance, each one needs consent, identification of the sender and a way to unsubscribe.

  • Express consent doesn't expire, though people can withdraw it at any time. Implied consent lasts for set periods, such as six months after an inquiry or two years after a purchase.
  • Unsubscribe requests must be processed without delay, and within 10 business days.
  • The sender has to prove consent, so keep the record for as long as you contact the person.
  • The maximum penalty per violation is $1 million for an individual and $10 million for a business.

In the CRM, that means a consent record on every person, with the type (express or implied), the date and where it came from; unsubscribes that stop every cadence at once; and texting through a tool that logs to Salesforce, never from a rep's own phone. My texting in Salesforce guide is written for US rules, so read its compliance notes with CASL in mind. None of this is legal advice.

Privacy

Personal information about sellers and buyers falls under Canada's private-sector privacy law, PIPEDA, or under a provincial law in Alberta, British Columbia and Quebec. For the CRM, the practical side is the same everywhere: collect what you need, limit who can export it, and be able to find and delete a person's information when you're asked to. Only owners and ops should be able to export lists. See VA access in Salesforce for how to limit what callers see.

Currency, provinces and time zones

  • Currency. Set the org's corporate currency to CAD. If you buy on both sides of the border, Salesforce's multiple currencies feature lets each deal carry its own currency. Once it's turned on it can't be turned off, so decide before you build.
  • Province on everything. Every lead, deal and campaign carries its province, with the city or market under it, so every report splits by province. My new market checklist covers the rest of a launch.
  • Time zones. Canada spans six of them. Route leads by province, and set callback due times and calling windows in the seller's local time.
  • Assignment and wholesaling rules. They differ by province, and some have changed recently. Track the province on every deal, record any disclosure or deadline your lawyer says applies, and report on deals missing it. The approach is the same one I describe for US states in tracking wholesaling rules in your CRM.

BRRRR investors: count the properties you keep

If you run BRRRR and keep properties as rentals, an ROI report that only counts assignment fees makes the campaigns that found your rentals look worse than they are. The ROI in my Monday 5 adds the value of kept properties for exactly this reason. Flag kept deals, give each one a value, and include them in campaign ROI. Does Salesforce work for Canadian real estate investors?

Yes. The deal flow is the same as in the US. The differences are in setup: CAD as the corporate currency, the province on every lead and deal, calling windows by time zone, and consent, do-not-call and unsubscribe records that match the CRTC's rules and CASL.

Do the National Do Not Call List rules apply to calling homeowners?

Ask a lawyer how they apply to your calls, and set up the CRM for them either way. According to the CRTC, telemarketers must register with the list, must not call listed numbers unless an exemption applies, must keep their own do-not-call list for 3 years and 14 days, and may call from 9:00 a.m. to 9:30 p.m. on weekdays and 10:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. on weekends.

Does CASL apply to texting sellers?

Texts and emails meant to get someone to do business with you are commercial electronic messages under CASL, which need consent, identification and an unsubscribe option. Record consent on every person in the CRM, process unsubscribes within 10 business days, and check the details with a lawyer.

Can Salesforce handle both CAD and USD?

Yes. Set CAD as the corporate currency, and turn on multiple currencies if you buy on both sides of the border, so each deal carries its own currency. Multiple currencies can't be turned off once enabled, so plan it before you build.
