Working with me

We're about to hire a full-time Salesforce admin. Should we?

Maybe later. At a team of 5 to 50, most of the work is small changes every week: a new lead source, a new hire, an alert that broke. A retainer covers that for less than a salary. Hire an admin or use a retainer compares the two. If you do hire, here's how to hire a Salesforce admin, with interview questions and a paid test.

We're adding reps or opening a new market. Should we fix the CRM first?

If you can, yes. More leads through the same leaks means more money lost the same way. Look at what your current sources bring back before you add more. Climbmark shows it free from your own CRM. For the setup itself, use the checklists for adding acquisitions reps and opening a new market.

We're on Podio, REsimpli or GoHighLevel. Can you help?

On GoHighLevel, connect it to Climbmark free and look back 13 weeks. You're one person. Why not an agency?

With an agency, your project often goes through a project manager to a consultant who may never have worked in real estate. With me, the person who answers your message is the person who builds it. Nothing in your org changes without your OK, and each quick fix (up to 3) is agreed with you first. Your data stays in your org, and I'm happy to sign your NDA before I log in.

About Salesforce

Is Salesforce worth it for a wholesaling or home-buying team?

It depends on your size and how custom your process is. Investor CRMs are faster to start with. Salesforce makes sense when you need your own pipeline, reports leadership trusts, or more integrations than an investor CRM allows. I wrote the honest version, including when Salesforce is overkill: Salesforce vs investor CRMs.

How do you set up speed to lead in Salesforce?

A Flow runs the moment a lead is created: it assigns the lead to the right rep or queue, creates a callback task and sends an alert. Missed calls on marketing numbers create their own callback tasks, and a report shows how long sellers really wait. The full setup is in the speed to lead guide.

How long does a Salesforce implementation take?

Most focused builds take 6 to 12 weeks, scoped up front: the design first, then the core build (lead routing, pipeline, automations and integrations), then reports and training. Bigger builds take longer and are scoped before they start.
