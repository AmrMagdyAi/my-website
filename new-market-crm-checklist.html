Opening a new market? Set up your CRM before the first mailer drops.

When a home-buying company opens a second market, the marketing usually goes live before the CRM is ready for it. Leads from both markets land in the same lists, reps get leads from a city they've never seen, and three months in, nobody can say whether the new market is making money. I run the Salesforce for a two-market home-buying company in Ohio, so this is the checklist I'd work through before the first mailer, ad or cold call goes out.

Short answer

Make market a field on every lead, deal, campaign and buyer, filled in automatically from the phone number, web form or zip code, never typed by a rep. Then add market to routing, spend tracking, underwriting assumptions, contract templates, buyer lists and every dashboard. Before launch, call every new number and submit every new form, and check that each test lead lands with the right market, source and owner.

What goes wrong when you add a market without changing the CRM?

Nothing breaks on day one, which is why it's easy to skip. The problems show up a few weeks later:

  • You can't tell if the new market is profitable. Spend, leads and contracts from both markets blend together, so the new market's numbers hide inside the old one's.
  • Leads go to the wrong people. Round robin doesn't know a lead is in a city your rep has never driven through.
  • Offers use the wrong assumptions. Repair costs, ARV comps and the offer formula from your first market don't fit the second.
  • Dispo blasts the wrong buyers. Buyers in one city get deals from another and start ignoring your emails.
  • Contracts go out on the wrong template. A new state can mean different contract language, disclosures and closing steps.

All of these are cheap to prevent before launch and expensive to untangle after, because by then you have months of leads with no market on them.

The new market checklist

1. Market on every record

  • A Market field on leads, deals, campaigns, buyers and transactions, with the same values everywhere
  • Filled automatically: from the tracking number the seller called, a hidden field on the web form, or the property's zip code or county
  • Reps can't leave it blank or change it without a reason
  • A report of records with no market, checked weekly until it stays at zero

In the org I run, leads, deals and campaigns all carry their market. That one field is what makes everything below possible.

2. Numbers, forms and lead sources

  • Every new call-tracking number mapped to its market and channel before it goes on a mailer or ad
  • Web forms for the new market send the market with the lead
  • PPC and social campaigns named with the market, so spend matches leads
  • New lead sources added to your picklists, not typed as free text

3. Spend by market

  • Monthly spend recorded per campaign and per market
  • The same split in your accounting (a class or location per market in QuickBooks, for example), so the CRM can be checked against the books
  • Cost per lead and cost per contract reported by market from day one

Campaign ROI by market and source, checked against the books, is part of what I built for the company I work with. Without the same split in accounting, you end up arguing with your own dashboard.

4. Routing and response

  • A queue or assignment rule entry per market, so new-market leads go to the people working that market
  • After-hours rules that follow the new market's clock if it's in another time zone
  • Missed-call alerts on every new marketing number
  • A "time until contacted" measure you can split by market

More on the routing side in Salesforce lead routing for real estate investors.

5. People and roles

  • Who owns a new-market lead from first call to appointment, written down
  • Field reps or appointment setters in the market set up as users, with access to what they need and nothing more
  • Appointments on a map, so you can see where people are driving
  • A manager who can see every lead in the market

6. Underwriting

  • ARV and maximum offer on the deal record, not in a spreadsheet
  • Repair cost assumptions and the offer percentage set per market, so the formula fits local numbers
  • Comps sources noted for the new market
  • An underwriting queue, so new-market deals get reviewed by someone who knows the area

The deal analyzer shows the offer math if you want to sanity-check the formula.

7. Contracts, title and closing

  • Contract templates for the new state in DocuSign or whatever you use, chosen automatically by the property's state
  • State disclosure requirements for wholesaling checked and tracked (see wholesaling laws and CRM tracking)
  • Title companies or closing attorneys for the new market on file, depending on how closings work in that state
  • Closing tasks adjusted for local steps

8. Dispositions

  • Buyers tagged by market, with the zip codes they buy in
  • Deal blasts filtered by market and zip
  • Buyer tiers based on what each buyer has actually bought, so your best buyers in the new market see deals first

Buyer tiers updated from repeat purchases and preferred zips are part of the dispo setup I run, and they matter more in a new market, where you don't know the buyers yet.

9. Reports and dashboards

  • A market filter on every dashboard the owner and managers use
  • Your weekly numbers split by market (the Monday 5 works well for this)
  • A market view: spend, leads, contacts, appointments, offers, contracts, closed deals and net profit per deal

10. Duplicates across markets

  • Duplicate matching on phone and email across all markets, not within each one
  • A rule for sellers who own property in both markets: one person, separate deals
  • When a duplicate comes in, the original owner is told instead of the lead being quietly reassigned

The launch test

The week before launch, do this with whoever runs your CRM:

  1. Call every new marketing number and let one call ring out.
  2. Submit every new web form.
  3. Check each test lead: right market, right source, right owner or queue, a task created, the missed-call alert fired.
  4. Move one test deal from offer to closing and check the right contract template and tasks appear.
  5. Open the dashboard filtered to the new market and make sure the test records show up.
  6. Delete the test records.

It takes about an hour, and it's the cheapest hour of the whole launch.

What should you measure in the first 90 days?

The same funnel you track in your first market, split by market: spend, leads, contact rate, appointments, offers, contracts, cost per contract and, once deals close, net profit per deal.

One thing that helps: compare the new market with how your first market did in its first three months, not with how it does today. A mature market has a buyer list, a reputation and reps who know the streets. A new market has none of that, and judging it against your best numbers can kill a market that was on track.

Decide up front what would make you keep going, change the channel mix or pull out, and write it down before the first mailer goes out. Otherwise the decision gets made on feel.

What changes for a virtual market?

If you're working the new market remotely, with local field reps or appointment setters, a few extra things matter:

  • Appointments assigned to a local person, with the address, time and seller notes on their phone before they get in the car.
  • Photos and documents on the deal, in a folder per deal that the whole team can see, not in a field rep's camera roll.
  • A clear handoff from the remote caller to the local rep and back, with a task at each step so nothing waits on a text message.
  • Local title or closing contacts on file, because nobody on your team can drive over to fix a problem.

Questions

How do I track marketing ROI by market?

Put a Market field on every lead, deal and campaign, filled automatically from the tracking number, web form or zip code. Record monthly spend per campaign and market, split the same way in your accounting, and report cost per contract and net profit per deal by market.

Should each market have its own Salesforce org?

Almost never for a 10 to 50 person company. One org with a market field on every record lets you share buyers, reports and automations while still splitting everything by market. Separate orgs make sense only for separate businesses.

How do I route leads by market in Salesforce?

Use a queue or an assignment rule entry per market, based on the Market field. Make sure the market is set before assignment runs, which is why it should come from the phone number, form or zip code rather than a rep.

How do I compare a new market with my existing one?

Compare it with your first market's first three months, not with your first market today. Track the same funnel by market: leads, contact rate, appointments, offers, contracts and cost per contract.

What changes in the CRM for a virtual wholesaling market?

Appointments assigned to local field reps, photos and documents stored on the deal, a tracked handoff between remote callers and local reps, and local title or closing contacts on file.

When should a new market get its own dashboard?

From day one, but as a filter on your existing dashboards rather than a separate set. Everyone looks at the same numbers, split by market, so the new market can't hide inside the old one.
