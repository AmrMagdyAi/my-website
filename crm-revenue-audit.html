Why I built this

The question I hear most

“We spend thousands a month on mail, cold calling and PPC. Which of it is actually working?”

It's a fair question, and the CRM should answer it in under a minute. Usually it can't, and not because anyone did anything wrong. Teams grow, people add a field here and a Zap there, and two years later the numbers on the dashboard are slightly off and nobody is quite sure why.

I've spent the last two years building and running the Salesforce system behind a home-buying company in Ohio, from the first call on a marketing number to the closing table. This audit is the checklist I wish someone had handed me on day one.

What I check

Six areas, one question each

Each area comes back with a straight answer, the evidence behind it, and what the fix would take.

01

Marketing and ROI

Is every lead tied to the campaign that paid for it? Do the deals you keep, like rentals, count toward that campaign's return?

Can you see cost per contract by channel, and trust it?

02

Speed to lead

What happens to a missed call, a weekend web form, a text reply? Who gets told, and how fast?

How many sellers waited, and for how long?

03

Acquisitions and follow-up

Offers with no activity, appointments with no next step, leads owned by someone who left. In states with new wholesaling rules, whether disclosures and cancellation dates are tracked. A tracking check, not legal advice.

Which deals are quietly going cold right now?

04

Data health

Broken phone numbers, duplicate sellers, deals with no lead behind them, lead sources anyone can change.

How much of your list can you actually reach?

05

Automations and integrations

Flows, Zaps, dialer and e-signature syncs, alerts. What fires, what fails quietly, what overlaps, and whether dialer and AI follow-up lands in the CRM with consent recorded.

What's running that nobody would notice if it stopped?

06

Reporting

The reports and dashboards you open every week, checked against the raw data underneath.

Do the five numbers you run the business on add up?

How it works

Five steps, two calls

About two hours of your time in total. I do the rest.

  1. Step 1Kick-off call

    You walk me through how a deal moves through your team, and what's bugging you.

    You: a 45-minute call and a read-only login (I send simple steps).

  2. Step 2The review

    I work through all six areas in your org. If I find something urgent, like an automation that's failing quietly, you hear about it the same day.

    You: nothing, unless I have a question.

  3. Step 3Quick fixes

    I send you the three small fixes I'd make first. Once you say yes, I make them, so you see results before the walkthrough.

    You: a yes or no on each fix.

  4. Step 4Report delivered

    The findings report, the roadmap and the dashboard spec arrive before we meet, so you can read them first.

    You: a read, at your own pace.

  5. Step 5Walkthrough

    We go through the findings with whoever on your team should be in the room, and agree what happens next.

    You: a 60-minute call, recorded.

Questions

Before you ask

What access do you need?

A read-only Salesforce user covers most of the review. You create it, so you can see what it does and switch it off at any time. Nothing in your org changes without your OK, and the three quick fixes are agreed with you first.

How long does it take?

It runs on your schedule: the kick-off call, my review, three agreed quick fixes, the report, then the walkthrough. Your part is about two hours in total, and we set the walkthrough date on the kick-off call.

Do we have to hire you for the fixes?

No. The roadmap is written so your team, or anyone you choose, can follow it. If you do hire me for the build within 30 days, the full audit fee comes off the project.

What if we're not on Salesforce?

Then the audit isn't the right step. You need a move to Salesforce: I map what you track today, move your data and build around how your team works. Book a free call and we'll scope it.

You're in Cairo. How does the time difference work?

I work Cairo afternoons and evenings, which is the US morning and the UK afternoon. Both calls are booked at a time that suits you.

How do we pay?

50% to start and 50% when the report is delivered, by bank transfer or card through an invoice. Prefer escrow? It can run as an Upwork contract instead, so your payment is held until you approve the report. The full scope and terms are on one page.

What if the audit doesn't find much?

Then you shouldn't pay for it. If the report doesn't give you at least five specific, fixable issues worth more than the fee, tell me on the walkthrough and I refund it in full. No forms, no argument.
