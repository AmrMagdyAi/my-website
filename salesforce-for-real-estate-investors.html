Salesforce for real estate investors and home-buying companies.

Salesforce makes sense for an investor team when deals leak between steps nobody owns: missed calls, follow-ups that stop at touch two, offers made outside the system, and profit nobody ties back to the campaign that paid for the lead. This page shows how a home-buying operation maps into Salesforce, the ten builds that pay for themselves, what it really costs in 2026, and when you should not use it.

Who this is for, and who it isn't

I build and run Salesforce for US home-buying companies. For the last two years I have built and run the system behind Central City Solutions, a home-buying company in Columbus, Ohio. Everything on this page comes from that kind of work. I sell Salesforce work, so weigh my view with that in mind, and read the "not for you" list twice.

Salesforce fits when

  • You have about three or more people touching leads (acquisitions reps, a lead manager, dispositions, a transaction coordinator) and need to see who owns what.
  • You market in more than one channel or market and want profit per campaign, not leads per campaign.
  • Your process is yours: your stages, your underwriting, your rules. Investor CRMs are built around a typical process; Salesforce is built around yours.
  • You already pay for several tools that don't talk to each other, and someone rebuilds the Monday numbers by hand.

Salesforce is overkill when

  • You are a solo wholesaler or a team of two. An investor CRM or a clean spreadsheet will do more for less.
  • Nobody will own the system. Without an operations person or outside help, a Salesforce org decays faster than a simpler tool.
  • You want dialer, list stacking, skip tracing and mail in one box. REsimpli bundles those; Salesforce needs separate tools connected to it.
  • Your current tool already answers "who owns this lead, what happened last, what happens next" and your weekly numbers come out without a spreadsheet.

The 10 builds that pay for themselves

These are the automations I'd build first for a team of 3 to 30. Each one closes a leak a reader can name. None of them needs code.

1. Missed-call alert

What it does
When a call from a seller number is logged as missed, Salesforce creates a callback task for the lead owner and pushes a notification to their phone.
Why it matters
A seller who reaches voicemail often calls the next buyer on the list, a point investors make in this BiggerPockets thread. This turns a missed call into a named job with a due time.
How it is built
A record-triggered flow on the Task (or the call-log object your phone system writes to) checks the call result. It creates a task due in minutes and fires a custom notification. Watch the limit: 10,000 notifications an hour per org, and Salesforce drops the extras rather than queueing them.

2. Shared lead queue

What it does
New seller leads land in a queue the whole acquisitions team can see, and the first rep to accept owns it.
Why it matters
Leads stop sitting in the inbox of the rep who is off today. Ownership is visible.
How it is built
A lead queue plus a lead assignment rule for web forms and imports. Bigger teams can route from the queue with Omni-Channel so reps get leads based on capacity. Only one lead assignment rule can be active at a time, so all routing logic lives in its entries.

3. Speed-to-lead clock

What it does
Each lead stores the minutes from created to first real conversation, by rep and by campaign.
Why it matters
You can't fix what you can't see, and without a field for it this number is a guess. See the speed to lead guide for why the first hour matters.
How it is built
A date-time field stamped by a flow when the first connected call or appointment is logged, and a formula field that turns it into minutes. A report groups it by owner and lead source.

4. Follow-up cadence that stops when the seller replies

What it does
Warm and cold sellers get dated follow-up tasks for weeks, and the cadence pauses when a conversation happens.
Why it matters
In my experience follow-up dies at touch two because nobody owns touch three. The cadence makes sure touch three and touch eight happen.
How it is built
A record-triggered flow with scheduled paths (for example day 3, day 14, day 45 after the last contact date). Changing the date reschedules the pending paths, so a new logged call pushes the next follow-ups back. Build your touch plan with the free cadence builder first.

5. Same seller, three lists

What it does
When a list import or a web form brings in an owner you already have, Salesforce flags it before a second rep starts calling.
Why it matters
Stacked lists repeat owners constantly. Two reps calling the same seller with two different offers costs the deal and your name.
How it is built
Matching and duplicate rules on the lead, matched on phone, email and a cleaned property address field. Gotcha: rules don't run on lead conversion or manual merge, and imports that hit a blocking rule fail silently, so check the import results file.

6. Campaign ROI on kept deals

What it does
Every closed deal carries the campaign that produced the first lead, and the report counts profit only from deals that closed or that you kept as rentals.
Why it matters
Cost per lead flatters the wrong channels. Cost per kept deal shows where to put next month's money. The Kept-Deal Map explains the idea.
How it is built
Lead source and campaign copy from the lead to the opportunity at conversion and lock after that. Actual profit lives on the closing record. A roll-up or flow sums profit back to the campaign, where campaign cost already sits. On Enterprise-level editions a joined report puts spend and profit side by side.

7. Underwriting on the opportunity

What it does
ARV, repair estimate, desired profit and the maximum allowable offer live on the deal, not in a side spreadsheet.
Why it matters
The offer and the math behind it stay together, so a manager can review the number and you can compare forecast with actual later.
How it is built
Currency fields for ARV and repairs, a formula for MAO (the same math as the free deal analyzer), and a screen flow that walks the rep through intake questions. Lock the contract price when the stage moves to Under Contract.

8. Transactions board

What it does
Every deal under contract shows on a board by closing stage: title opened, inspection, buyer assigned, clear to close, closed.
Why it matters
Deals die between contract and closing when nobody calls the seller. A board makes the stuck ones obvious.
How it is built
A custom Transaction object with a stage picklist and a Kanban list view. Kanban shows up to 200 cards per view, so filter by market or closing month if you run more.

9. Buyer matching

What it does
When a deal is ready for dispositions, Salesforce lists the buyers whose buy box fits: area, price band, property type, rehab level.
Why it matters
Blasting every deal to the whole list burns buyers. Sending it to the ten who fit gets faster answers. The free cash buyers list template has the fields.
How it is built
Buy-box fields on the buyer Contact, and a flow or report that filters buyers against the deal's zip, price and type. Record who was sent the deal and who replied, so the list learns who really buys.

10. Signed contract alert

What it does
When the seller signs, the deal moves to Under Contract, the contract date is stamped and the team is told.
Why it matters
Nobody has to remember to update the stage, so the contract-to-first-call clock in your Monday numbers starts on time.
How it is built
An e-signature integration (I use DocuSign) writes the envelope status to Salesforce; a record-triggered flow updates the stage, stamps the date and notifies the transaction coordinator.

What it really costs

Licenses are the smallest part of the bill. Salesforce pricing depends on the edition and the number of users, and most businesses can get a discount through their Salesforce account manager, so get a quote for your team before you compare. What matters more is which edition your plan needs, because the limits below decide what you can build. In September 2026 Salesforce introduced Core, Advanced and Max and positions Core against the older Enterprise edition (announcement); Salesforce Help still documents limits under the older names.

EditionWhat matters for an investor team
Starter SuiteNo custom objects (limits), so no Property or Transaction records. Fine for trying Salesforce, not for running acquisitions to closing.
Pro Suite50 custom objects, but 5 flows in total (limits). API access is an add-on, and most phone and e-signature integrations need it, so ask for it in the quote.
CoreSalesforce's Enterprise-level edition. Help documents 2,000 active flows for Enterprise (limits), plus joined reports and approvals. Salesforce lists Premier Success and 500K Flex Credits a year with Core; confirm with your rep.
Advanced / MaxMore AI, security and data tools; Max includes Salesforce Maps and Agentforce for Sales. Rarely needed under 30 users.

What goes into year one

Ask your Salesforce account manager to quote the edition your plan needs for your number of users, with API access if you connect a phone or e-signature tool. On top of the licenses come the build (scoped per project; my CRM Revenue Audit is a fixed $1,500 and ends with a roadmap you can build with anyone), your phone and texting provider, and whoever owns the system after launch.

GoHighLevel's Unlimited plan is $297 a month with unlimited users, plus usage fees for calls and texts (GoHighLevel pricing). If those tools answer your Monday questions, they are cheaper. Questions

Is Salesforce worth it for a real estate investor, or is it too expensive?

It is worth it when a team of roughly three or more people loses deals between steps and needs reporting its current tool can't give. License cost depends on the edition and the number of users, and most businesses can get a discount through their Salesforce account manager. In year one, the build and upkeep often cost more than the licenses. A solo wholesaler is usually better served by an investor CRM.

Which Salesforce edition should a wholesaler or home-buying company buy?

Starter Suite can't hold custom objects, so it can't store properties or transactions. Pro Suite works for a small team but caps you at 5 flows and leaves out joined reports and approvals. Teams that want the automations on this page usually need an Enterprise-level edition, which Salesforce now sells as Core. Check current limits with your Salesforce rep before you sign.

Can Salesforce replace REsimpli or Podio completely?

It can replace the CRM part: leads, deals, tasks, reporting and automation. List stacking, skip tracing, direct mail and the dialer come from separate tools connected to Salesforce. If you want all of that in one box with no admin, an investor CRM is the simpler choice.

Do I need a full-time Salesforce admin?

Not for a team of 3 to 30, but someone has to own it. That can be an operations person who handles small changes, with outside help for builds, or an ongoing management agreement. A Salesforce org nobody owns slowly fills with unused fields and broken automations.

How do sellers, properties and deals fit in Salesforce?

A seller inquiry starts as a Lead. When it becomes a real deal, it converts into an Account and Contact for the seller and an Opportunity for the deal. A custom Property object holds the address and property facts, so a repeat seller with a second house gets a second deal without a duplicate person.

Does Salesforce have a dialer and texting for cold calling?

Calling and texting usually come from a connected phone system that logs calls and messages to Salesforce. I have built RingCentral call sync, and other dialers offer Salesforce integrations too. Calling and texting sellers is regulated: the FCC's telemarketing rules (TCPA) apply to calls and texts, and business texting follows carrier rules, including A2P 10DLC registration (see CTIA's messaging principles). Check both with your provider and counsel.

How long does it take to set up Salesforce for a home-buying company?

It depends on how many tools you connect and how clean your data is. The plan on this page runs about 90 days from audit to full reporting, with the first useful pieces (lead intake, queue, missed-call alert) live in the first month. Treat that as a planning example, not a promise.

Can Salesforce show which marketing campaign produced each closed deal?

Yes, if the campaign travels from the lead to the opportunity at conversion and is locked after that, and if closed profit is recorded on the deal. Then a report shows profit per campaign against spend. Most setups break this chain at conversion or at closing.

What happens to my data if I leave Salesforce?

You can export your data as CSV files with the Data Export Service. Per Salesforce, weekly exports are available on Enterprise, Performance and Unlimited, and Professional-level orgs can export every 29 days. Data Loader can export larger sets where API access is included.

Can AI answer seller calls or write follow-ups in Salesforce?

Salesforce sells Agentforce for this (Agentforce). Pricing depends on how you use it and on your edition, so ask your Salesforce account manager for a quote. For most investor teams, the bigger win is fixing ownership and follow-up first; AI on top of a leaky process answers faster and still loses the deal.
