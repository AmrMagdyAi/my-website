How to find cash buyers for wholesale deals: find them, verify them, keep them in the CRM

Search for how to find cash buyers and you get the same list: a data tool, a real estate investor association, the MLS, auctions, a classified ad. Those are real places to find names. What the lists skip is everything after the name: a person who paid cash once is not yet a buyer for your deal, and a name in a spreadsheet nobody owns goes stale in a quarter. This guide covers where wholesalers find cash buyers, the checks that turn a name into a buyer you can send a deal to, the fields to record, and the rules that keep the list alive.

Short answer

Find buyers in six places: a data tool's cash buyer list, county deed and sales records, investor association meetings, agents and property managers who work with investors, auctions, and other wholesalers. Then verify each one before you count it: recent purchases in your price band and area, proof of funds, a stated buy box and a working phone or email. Record source, buy box, verification date and owner in the CRM, send only matching deals, and retire buyers who stop answering.

Why is a name not a buyer?

  • "Cash buyer" is a label, not a promise. A tool marks someone who paid cash for a property. It does not know whether they want your deal.
  • Buy boxes differ. A flipper who buys $180,000 three-bedrooms in two zip codes is no use for a $60,000 duplex across the county.
  • Lists decay. Investors change markets, run out of funds or stop answering. A list nobody maintains is a list of the people who used to buy.
  • The cost of a bad list shows up late. You send the deal to 200 names, get no offers and lose days on a contract with a closing date. The fix is a smaller list you know.

How do you verify a buyer before you count them?

  1. Recent purchases. At least one purchase in the last six to twelve months in your area, and the address and price in the record. Your own window may be shorter or longer.
  2. Price band and property type. Which kinds of deals they bought, not which they say they like.
  3. Funds. Ask for proof of funds or a lender letter once they're serious, and note the date you saw it. Funds change.
  4. Buy box in their words. Areas, condition they'll take, how fast they close, whether they take assignments or only direct purchases.
  5. A live contact. A call or email that gets an answer, with the consent you need for texting. Record the date you reached them.

Until a buyer passes at least the first and last checks, keep the record as "unverified" and out of deal sends. A shorter list of verified buyers does more than a long list of names.

What should the CRM record about each buyer?

  • Who: person and entity, phone, email, preferred contact method and time, consent to be texted, with the date.
  • Source: where you found them and who found them. This is how you learn which source brings buyers who close.
  • Buy box: markets or zip codes, property types, price range, condition tolerance, funding type (cash, hard money) and closing speed.
  • Verification: status (unverified, verified, retired), date, last purchase seen with address and price, proof of funds seen with date.
  • Activity: deals sent, deals they offered on, deals they closed on with you, last contact date, owner.
  • Do not send flag and the reason, so a buyer who asked to stop is never emailed again.

The fields are the same ones the cash buyers list template starts from; the matching and sending are covered in wholesale dispositions in Salesforce.

What about duplicates and LLC names?

Investors buy in the names of LLCs, and the same investor can appear under three entities and two phone numbers. Match on phone, email and mailing address before you match on name, and keep the person as the record that owns the entities. It is the same problem as duplicate sellers: a buyer split across records gets the same deal three times and has no history.

How do you keep the list alive?

  1. Send only matching deals. A buyer who gets ten deals that don't fit stops reading the eleventh.
  2. Tier by results, not by hope. Buyers who closed with you first, buyers who offered next, verified buyers who haven't offered after that.
  3. Track response. Record which deals a buyer opened, answered or ignored.
  4. Retire on a rule. For example, no response to five matching deals in a row moves a buyer to "retired", not deleted.
  5. Re-verify on a schedule. A quarterly check of the top tier, so proof of funds and buy box are current.
  6. Respect the channel rules. Calls, texts and emails to buyers are marketing contact too, so consent, opt-outs and do-not-call rules still apply. This is information, not legal advice.

Which numbers should you watch?

  • Verified buyers by market and by price band, so you can see where you have no one to send a deal to.
  • Buyers by source, and closings by source.
  • Deals sent per buyer and offers per deal sent.
  • Days from contract to first offer, which feeds your assignment fee and your risk on a short closing window.

If a market has strong seller flow and a thin buyer list, fix the buyer side before you buy more leads.

Questions

How do I find cash buyers for wholesale deals?

Use a data tool's cash buyer list, county deed and sales records, investor association meetings, agents and property managers who work with investors, auctions and other wholesalers. Then verify each buyer's recent purchases, price band, funds and buy box before you count them.

How do I know if a cash buyer is real?

Check at least one purchase in your area in the last six to twelve months, ask for proof of funds or a lender letter, get their buy box in their own words and confirm a working phone or email. Record each check and its date in the CRM.

What should be in a cash buyers list?

Contact and consent, source, buy box (areas, property types, price range, condition, funding, closing speed), verification status and date, last purchase seen, deals sent and closed, last contact and a do-not-send flag.

Is a cash buyers list from a tool enough?

It is a starting list of names. Tools mark recent cash purchasers, but they don't know whether someone wants your deal, so verify each name, record the buy box and keep only matching deals going out.

How many cash buyers do I need?

There is no standard number. What matters is verified buyers for each market and price band you buy in. If a deal in a band has no one to send it to, that band is where to build next.

How do I stop buyers getting deals that don't fit?

Record each buyer's buy box in fields, match each deal to them by area, type and price, and send only matches. Retire buyers who stop answering matching deals so the list stays small and accurate.
