Salesforce vs HubSpot for real estate investors and wholesalers

HubSpot comes up a lot when investor teams outgrow spreadsheets or an all-in-one tool, usually because someone on the team used it at a previous job. It's a good CRM. The question is whether it fits a business where the records that matter are properties, offers, contracts and buyers, as well as contacts and deals. I build Salesforce for investor teams, so weigh my view with that in mind. I've tried to give HubSpot its due.

Short answer

HubSpot is faster to set up and easier to run, and its prices are public. It fits a small, marketing-led team with a simple pipeline. The catch for investors is data structure: HubSpot's custom objects, which you'd want for properties, transactions and buyers, are an Enterprise feature. Salesforce takes more setup and needs someone to run it, but it lets you model the business as it is, automate deeply and report on anything. Around 10 people and up, with several markets or heavy reporting needs, Salesforce usually fits better.

Where HubSpot wins

  • Speed to start. A small team can be working in HubSpot within days, without a consultant.
  • Ease of use. Reps tend to find it friendly, and adoption is easier when the tool feels simple.
  • Public, predictable pricing. You know the bill before you talk to anyone.
  • Marketing, if you're inbound-heavy. Teams that live on SEO, email nurture and landing pages get a lot from HubSpot's marketing side, which is priced separately.
  • Calling minutes included on paid Sales Hub tiers, which is enough for some teams' follow-up, though most wholesaling teams run a dedicated dialer anyway.

Where Salesforce wins

  • Your business, modeled as it is. A seller can have several properties, a property several offers, a deal one buyer and one closing. Salesforce handles that with custom objects from Pro Suite up. In HubSpot you either wait for Enterprise or squeeze it into contacts and deals.
  • Automation depth. Missed-call alerts, callback tasks, follow-up that stops when a seller replies, transaction tasks by stage, buyer matching. Flow handles all of it, with a lot of room on Enterprise-level editions.
  • Reporting. Cost per contract by campaign, ROI on kept deals, the owner's weekly numbers split by market. Custom report types and joined reports make the hard ones possible.
  • Integrations. Most dialers, texting tools, e-signature and accounting tools that investor teams use connect to Salesforce, and the AppExchange covers the rest.
  • Permissions. Fine control over who sees and edits what, which matters once you have VAs, several markets and a buyers list worth protecting.
  • Investor CRMs built on it. Some investor-focused CRMs run on Salesforce, so there's an investor-ready starting point on the same platform if you want one.

The cost question, honestly

HubSpot's price is public. For a made-up 10-seat team, Professional billed annually is 10 × $90 × 12 = $10,800 a year plus $1,500 onboarding, and Enterprise is 10 × $150 × 12 = $18,000 a year plus $3,500. Those are HubSpot's published per-seat prices; your real quote can differ.

Salesforce is quoted, and I don't publish list prices because they change and are negotiable. Most businesses can get a discount through their Salesforce account executive, depending on the business and the number of users. Salesforce also needs someone to set it up and run it, which is a real cost. I cover it in choosing a Salesforce consultant.

The fair comparison isn't Starter against Starter. It's the HubSpot tier that has what your plan needs (often Enterprise, for custom objects) against the Salesforce edition that has what your plan needs (often Enterprise-level, for automation). The edition picker helps with the Salesforce side.

Can you run both?

Yes. Some teams keep HubSpot for marketing email and landing pages and use Salesforce as the record of every seller and deal. HubSpot has a Salesforce integration for this. The rule that keeps it sane: decide which system creates the person and which owns each field, and sync on one key so you don't create duplicates. My duplicate sellers guide covers the Salesforce side.

If you're moving from HubSpot to Salesforce, the steps are the same as any move: map the fields, decide which history comes across, rebuild the automations that matter and cut over on one day. The switch guide has a field map and a 30-60-90 day plan.

Questions

Is HubSpot good for real estate investors?

For a small, marketing-led team with a simple pipeline, yes. It's quick to set up and easy to use. It gets harder when you need properties, transactions and buyers as separate records, because custom objects are an Enterprise feature in HubSpot.

Is Salesforce or HubSpot better for wholesaling?

For a team of roughly 10 or more with several lead sources, markets and a dispositions side, Salesforce usually fits better because it can model properties, offers, contracts and buyers and report across them. For a small team, HubSpot or an all-in-one investor CRM is simpler.

How much does HubSpot cost for a real estate team?

HubSpot's published Sales Hub prices per seat per month are $9 annual or $15 monthly on Starter, $90 annual or $100 monthly on Professional plus $1,500 onboarding, and $150 on Enterprise plus $3,500 onboarding, as of its September 2026 pricing article. Check HubSpot's pricing page before you buy.

Does HubSpot have custom objects for properties?

Only on Enterprise. HubSpot's knowledge base lists custom objects as an Enterprise feature in any hub, so on Starter or Professional properties have to live in deals or contacts.

Can I use HubSpot and Salesforce together?

Yes. Some teams keep HubSpot for marketing and use Salesforce for sellers and deals, connected through HubSpot's Salesforce integration. Decide which system creates each person and owns each field, and sync on one key to avoid duplicates.

How hard is it to move from HubSpot to Salesforce?

It's a normal CRM move: map fields, decide which history to bring, rebuild key automations and cut over all forms and numbers on one day. Plan for activity history that may not export cleanly, and run a small test import first.
