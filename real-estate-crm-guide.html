What is a real estate CRM?

A CRM (customer relationship management system) is a database plus rules. For a real estate investor, the database holds sellers, properties, offers, contracts and buyers; the rules decide who calls a lead, when, and what must be captured before a lead moves forward. A real estate agent CRM is built around buyers and listings; an investor CRM is built around motivated sellers, offers and dispositions. That difference is why generic advice often doesn’t fit wholesalers.

When does an investor need a CRM?

When a lead can be forgotten and nobody notices. In practice: more than one person talks to sellers, follow-ups depend on memory, or you can’t say which list paid for your last deal. The 7-sign checklist walks through it, including what a spreadsheet still does well.

What should a real estate CRM automate?

  • New lead → assigned owner and a call task due within minutes. Contacting within an hour made firms nearly 7× as likely to qualify a lead.
  • Missed inbound call → callback task plus a text, if you have consent. Only 2% of unanswered callers leave a voicemail.
  • No activity in 7 days → follow-up task for the owner.
  • “Not now” → re-contact date and a long-term cadence, not a dead lead.
  • Under contract → title task, dispo alert and buyer list match.
  • Offer above MAO → manager approval.
  • Duplicate lead → alert and merge, keeping the first source.
  • Monday → the five KPI numbers emailed to the owner.

What compliance rules affect an investor’s CRM?

  • Texting: app-to-person texts over 10DLC numbers in the US require brand and campaign registration, per Twilio. CTIA’s guidance calls for express written consent for marketing texts and advises against rented, sold or shared lists. Store consent and opt-out on the lead. Texting guide
  • Calling: keep DNC flags from your list or skip-tracing provider on the record, and make every dialer respect them.
  • Platform changes: Salesforce’s Open CTI, used by many dialers, reaches end of life on February 28, 2028. Dialer guide

This is not legal advice; state wholesaling and telemarketing rules vary.

How do you set up a real estate CRM in 30 days?

Week 1: Decide the model

Write your stages on one page, with the fields required to leave each one. Pick lead source values. Decide who owns which leads.

Week 2: Build and import

Create fields, stages and rules. Clean and import one list first; the Data Import Wizard handles up to 50,000 records at a time in Salesforce. Check duplicates before the rest.

Week 3: Connect and automate

Phone and texting first, then web forms and lead sources. Turn on the new-lead task, missed-call task and no-activity sweep.

Week 4: Train and measure

Train on the five things each role does daily. Start the Monday review with the five numbers. Fix the field people skip most. See getting your team to use it.

Questions

What is the best CRM for real estate investors?

It depends on team size and how custom your process is. Investor-specific CRMs are fastest to start; Salesforce fits teams that need their own data model, deep automation and custom reports. Compare on data model, follow-up automation, phone integration and reporting, not feature counts.

What should a real estate investor CRM track?

Sellers with lead source and next follow-up date, properties, every offer, contracts, buyers with buy boxes, campaigns with spend, and every call and text with an outcome.

How is an investor CRM different from a realtor CRM?

A realtor CRM centers on buyers, listings and the sphere of influence. An investor CRM centers on motivated sellers, offers, contracts, dispositions to cash buyers and cost per deal by lead source.

How long does it take to set up a real estate CRM?

A usable core (stages, required fields, lead sources, phone logging and the key automations) can be live in about 30 days. Reports and extra integrations come after the team is using it.

Can a CRM help with speed to lead?

Yes. Automatic assignment and a task due within minutes for every new lead, plus a callback task for missed calls, make response time a process instead of a habit.
